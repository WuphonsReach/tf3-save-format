# Drawing the map

[tools/terrain_map.py](../tools/terrain_map.py) turns the terrain record and heightmap of [terrain.md](terrain.md) into a picture: SVG by default, with the terrain as vector bands and height lines, and the road and rail edges of [road-network.md](road-network.md) on top. This note holds what the tool's output was checked against and the choices that follow from the formats. The flags are in [tools/README.md](../tools/README.md).

## In short

- **The tool found the heightmap and gave a sensible picture in one catalog save of each of 568, 585, 599, 601 and 604 and in our own 604 saves (Observed).** The test of the frame is the roads: towns lie on land, roads keep out of the lakes and follow valleys, with x right, y up, not flipped.
- **The vector terrain is the water below `waterLevel` as one even-odd shape (rivers included), a filled band every 20 m above it, and a height line at each band's edge (Observed, 604).** It is small (0.34 MB for a 14,336 m map with bands, against 81 MB for the raster SVG of a Giant map) but has no hill shading.
- **Roads and tracks are drawn as wide as the sum of their own lanes, in metres (a small town street 16 m, a track 4 m);** a whole-map picture shows a track as a hairline unless `--min-width` raises the floor.
- **A map drawn with only part of its roads, the rest in dashes, means the edge reader stopped early (Observed, 8,043 of 21,154 records until the fixed 16 MiB limit was removed).**
- **The SVG records its placement in `<metadata>` (map size, metres per pixel, top-left corner, covered range, which way north is drawn, the crop), so a compass word read off a picture can be tied to its frame.**

## Evidence and tests

### Drawing the map

[tools/terrain_map.py](../tools/terrain_map.py) turns the terrain record and heightmap above into a picture (water below `waterLevel`, coloured height bands and height lines) with the road and rail edges of [road-network.md](road-network.md) on top. The default is an SVG with everything as vector paths in map metres: the terrain as water, bands and height lines, the network a path per edge in a layer per type (with data attributes). Edges with an end node in the save's free-id list, a deleted town's or road's leftovers, are left out unless `--keep-dead` is given ([town-leftovers.md](town-leftovers.md), 604). `--raster` draws the terrain as the shaded PNG it used before (height ramp, hill shading), embedded in the SVG, and `--png` gives one flat shaded PNG. The picture is north up, in the game's own frame (up is +y, right is +x) unless `--north sun` turns the grid a quarter turn ([compass.md](compass.md)). **Observed**: it found the heightmap and gave a sensible picture in one catalog save of each of 568, 585, 599, 601 and 604 (mod.io 6090964, 6187236, 6386889, 6401182, 6415512) and in our own 604 saves. The map sizes seen are in [map-sizes.md](map-sizes.md); the tile count was the metres divided by 256 each time. The test of the frame is the roads: in a 568 map of 14,336 m a side (mod.io 6098954), towns lie on land, roads keep out of the lakes and follow valleys, and the orientation of [the layout in terrain.md](terrain.md#the-heightmap) (x right, y up, not flipped) put them there. The picture covers exactly the map or the crop: a grid of n + 1 points a side is n pixels, grid point (i, j) at the top-left corner of pixel (i, j), so at the default 4 m per pixel a Small map is 1,152 x 3,456 pixels. A picture whose `generator-commit` is 2102fb8 or older is one pixel wider and taller, and if cropped also kept the edges and vehicles in that extra strip. In a flat PNG a station yard or turntable is unreadable at 8 m per pixel (a turntable's 20 stalls merge into one red blob), and needs `--crop X,Y,R` with `--mpp 2` or `1`; on a crop `--mpp` below 4 only interpolates the terrain. Each road and track is drawn as wide as its template's profile, in metres ([profile widths](street-templates.md#profile-widths), read from the install when the script runs; a small town street is 16 m, a track 4 m, and 16 and 4 for a type it cannot find). So a close crop shows them at their size, and a picture of a whole map shows a track as a hairline unless `--min-width` raises the floor. The SVG also carries a compass rose in a layer of its own, lower right unless `--rose` says otherwise, whose north arrow follows `--north` ([compass.md](compass.md)). `--grid N` adds a layer of crosses every N metres of map x and y, with a line of dots and a label at each multiple of 1000 m (the map's centre is 0, 0, x to the right and y up), to read a place's coordinates for `--crop`; `--grid-lines dots|faint` also draws the minor lines, `none` leaves out the dotted lines. In the SVG the network is vector and stays sharp at any zoom, so only the terrain behind it blurs. The vector terrain (**Observed**, one catalog save each of 604 and our own, 604) is the water below `waterLevel` as one even-odd shape (rivers included, since the game carves them below the water level, so one cut at the water level draws lakes, sea and rivers, and an island is a ring inside a ring), a filled band for every 20 m above the water (`--contour`), painted from the lowest up with a height ramp's colours, and a height line at each band's edge, every fifth heavier. It has no hill shading, so relief reads from the bands and lines and not from the lit slopes of the PNG, and the file is small: a 14,336 m map at 8 m is 0.18 MB without bands and 0.34 MB with them, and the 20 x 41 km Giant map 0.6 MB, where the raster SVG of that map is 81 MB. The lines are cut from the grid by linear interpolation along the cell edges, so a point on a line is at the stated height to within the thinning tolerance, and on a crop the cut uses the 4 m grid whatever `--mpp` is. The SVG also records its placement in a `<metadata>` element (map size, metres per pixel, the map point at the top-left corner, the covered x and y range, which way north is drawn, the crop), and a flat PNG carries the same record as JSON in a text chunk, so a compass word read off a picture ("the north building") can be tied to its frame, described in [tools/README.md](../tools/README.md). A labelled close-up (save 6425796, 604): [sample SVG](samples/terrain-map-area-labelled-6425796.svg).

A map drawn with only part of its roads and rails, the rest broken into dashes with pieces missing, means the edge reader stopped early: in a 604 map with a 946 MB stream it read 8,043 of 21,154 records until its fixed 16 MiB limit was removed ([road-network.md](road-network.md#the-street-edge-table)).
