# Running costs and upkeep

How the money journal books the upkeep of buildings and the running costs of vehicles (ships: [ship-running-costs.md](ship-running-costs.md)), and how those bookings match the yearly figures in the game's windows. How to find the journal, its periods and the Finances tab rows are in [finances.md](finances.md); a warehouse's upkeep per module is in [warehouses.md](warehouses.md#cost), and costs that rise with the date in [inflation.md](inflation.md#the-year-cost-table). Checked on 604 in one game, the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game).

## In short

- **How it is booked.** Upkeep and vehicle running costs are booked in batches every 60,000 clock units, so 24.35 batches make a year of 1,461,000 (Observed, 604, one game; the warehouse sum matched its window to within rounding). A building's window yearly figure divided by 24.35 is its booking per batch (Observed, 604; matched for depots, stations, warehouses and ports).
- **Where a building's figure comes from.** The base is in the building's file (`maintenanceCost`), scaled by the Infrastructure Upkeep setting; the window adds an accumulated part that vehicles passing a depot or maintenance building build up. The accumulated part is booked as vehicle maintenance at the next batch and starts again (Confirmed, 604, three buildings). What adds to it is Open.
- **A `modular_terminal` cargo station.** Upkeep is two platform modules plus one entrance, so a station with specialized platforms is 21,000 a year and a universal one 11,000 (Confirmed as arithmetic on the files, 604; the 21,000 read in windows, the 11,000 not). An extra terminal adds 20,000 and an extra entrance 1,000 (Observed, one station).
- **Ships.** A ship's running cost is booked per batch like the rest and depends on whether it is parked and on its condition: see [ship-running-costs.md](ship-running-costs.md).
- **Not known.** What adds to a building's accumulated cost; whether a depot also takes the year table on top of its file's era factors; why the booking order stops matching vehicles for the ships bought later (see below).

## Evidence and tests

### Upkeep and running costs are booked in batches (604)

**Observed** on 604 in the save of [the journal after ten minutes](finances-tests.md#loan-payments-and-income-over-ten-minutes) (clock 569,800).

- **Upkeep comes in a batch every 60,000 clock units.** There were 9 batches (at 60,000 to 540,000), each of 20 bookings: 11 `MAINTENANCE` / `INFRASTRUCTURE` (5 road, 5 water, 1 carrier `OTHER` for the warehouse), 2 vehicle maintenance (one road, one water) and 7 vehicle running costs (one per vehicle). The period is in clock units, not days: the date did not move while these were booked. The sums per batch ran from -23,886 to -25,158.
- **A batch is a fraction of a year.** The warehouse's window showed $50,000/Year, and its booking per batch (carrier `OTHER`) was -2,053. A year is 1,461,000 units ([finances.md](finances.md#periods)), so 24.35 batches make a year, and 24.35 x 2,053 = 49,990, within rounding of the window. **Observed**, one warehouse. The same window showed 21 fish in stock with 18 incoming and 8 outgoing in its chart, which did not add up at first (18 - 8 = 10). The stream held 29 unloaded and 8 loaded, so the stock of 21 was right, and the chart bar left out the newest entry, see [statistics-bars.md](statistics-bars.md#a-small-new-game-one-fish-line-three-windows-604). A later save showed 18 in stock with 30 incoming and 12 outgoing, which add up. **Observed**.
- **Per-booking values.** Infrastructure and vehicle maintenance repeated exactly in every batch (for example a water depot -4,106, road vehicles -315 in total, water vehicles -1,755). Running costs varied between batches: a ship booked -1,752 in most, -1,472, -1,402 or -1,262 in some, and a horse cart (`horsewagon_1850_v2` and `horsewagon_1850_usa_v2`) -284 to -417. The bookings carry no vehicle or line id (the five category bytes are all there is), but within a batch they come in vehicle order: summed over the nine batches, the fourth water running-cost booking came to 15,138, which is exactly Ship 4's last-year running costs in its window (the first three summed to 15,278, 14,998 and 14,508). **Observed**, one ship, but a match to the dollar. So a vehicle's yearly running costs can be rebuilt from the journal by its place in the batch. A save 62 s later (one more batch) gave Ship 3 16,260 and Ship 4 16,890 in the ships' windows, again the sums of the third and fourth water bookings (the batch added -1,752 to each). The rule did not hold for the two ships bought later: at 1,080,000 the water bookings were -1,682, -1,472, -1,752, -1,752, -454 and -2,814, but the window of the fifth ship (a larger ship standing in its depot) read -2,054 for last year, which is none of them, and the sixth ship was not looked at. So the order of the bookings is the vehicle order only for the first four ships, or the window adds something the bookings do not (it has no income). **Open**. Why one batch's cost for a vehicle differs from another's is partly explained in [ship-running-costs.md](ship-running-costs.md): a ship parked in a depot pays 5%, and the cost steps up with the vehicle's condition. One more batch in the next save (at 600,000) booked -1,122 for the second ship, the one the player had just seen leave the port, and -1,752 for the other three, which fits a ship standing at a port costing less, but it is one instance (the other low values were -1,612, -1,542 and -1,472, none matched to a known state).

### A road depot's upkeep against its window (604)

**Observed** on 604, the same game at clock 5,997,000 (save `1246`). The Bromsgrove Road Depot's window showed "Maintenance Pool 5 / 12", five maintained horse carts (Road Vehicles 8 to 12; the stone line's carts were `horsewagon_1850_usa_v2` and `horse_cart_small`) and a running cost of $30,503/Year.

- **The game's depot file gives 30,000.** The road depot in `depots/road.zip` (`road/road_depot/road_depot.con.lua`, in the base content) has a maintenance cost of 30,000 and a price of 180,000 for the first template (built up to 1939), both multiplied by 3 from 1940 and 9 from 1980. Those are the file's own era templates, not the steps of the year cost table (×2.2 at 1940, ×4.0 at 1980, [inflation.md](inflation.md#the-year-cost-table)); whether a depot also takes the table's multiplier is **Open**. Its script (`road_depot.script.lua`) sets a maintenance pool of 12, the "/ 12" in the window; the water depot has a pool of 8, 100,000 upkeep and 600,000 price.
- **The journal has a booking for it.** The batch at 5,940,000 holds two `INFRASTRUCTURE` (Road) bookings of -1,232. At 24.35 batches a year that is 29,999, so about $30,000 a year each, the file's value. The same batch's other infrastructure bookings fit their windows and files the same way: -1,724 (41,979, the Bromsgrove Station's $42,000/Year), 2 x -2,053 (49,991, the warehouses' $50,000/Year), 2 x -4,106 (99,981, the water depots' 100,000). Further values fit round yearly figures too: -862 (20,990), -985 (23,985), -616 (15,000), -347 (8,450) and 31 pieces of -205 (4,992, 24 `Road` and 7 `Tram`, mostly the maintenance buildings' halves, see below).
- **The first depot's booking was found by subtraction.** In the earliest merged month ([rows](finances.md#rows-against-the-booking-categories); the `Road` bookings are in [the first build-out](finances-tests.md#a-first-build-out-row-by-row)) the `Road` infrastructure total was -4,186 for two batches, so -2,093 a batch. That is a depot -1,232 plus a stop -862 (21,000 a year), the only two road buildings the new game started with. **Observed**.
- **The window says 30,503, not 30,000.** No booking is 1,253 (30,503 / 24.35). The window is 503 (1.7%) above the file's figure; the next bullet explains the extra as an accumulated part. The other road depot's window was not opened. The `Tram` bookings are not depot upkeep: a depot's construction cost is booked half as `Road` and half as `Tram` (depots at 974,000, 2,678,500 and 2,800,250), but its upkeep shows no `Tram` half.
- **The window's figure is the base plus an accumulated part.** In the game's GUI code (`gui/entity_window/entity_window_util.tl` in `base/content/gui.zip`) an entity's upkeep line is the `maintenanceCost` of its maintenance component, times the Infrastructure Upkeep scale (and the year multiplier for buildings that have a year progression), plus the sum of that component's `accumulatedCost` values. A building with nothing accumulated shows its base alone. The road maintenance building has a base of 10,000, a pool of 16 and a catchment radius of 600 (`road/road_maint_station.con.lua` and `.script.lua` in `depots/road.zip`); the road depot's catchment radius is 400. In the paused save the windows read: depot (5 vehicles listed) $30,503, two maintenance buildings at Todmorden (4 and 6 vehicles) $10,759 and $11,242, and the maintenance buildings at Retford and Bromsgrove (no vehicles) exactly $10,000. So the extra over the base is 503, 759 and 1,242 where vehicles are listed and 0 where none are, which fits an accumulated part that vehicles add. **Observed**, five windows. The player's reading of the vehicle list is that it holds the vehicles inside the building's catchment circle, up to the pool limit, and that the accumulated cost may build up as vehicles pass in and out of the circle and then reset until more are repaired. The link to the "vehicle maintenance" bookings is **Confirmed** by the next batch, see the last bullet below. What adds to the accumulated part is **Open**, and the accumulated values were not found in the save (a search for the figures as doubles gave only chance hits).
- **The maintenance buildings' base upkeep is booked as a `Road` and a `Tram` half.** The batch holds no -411 (10,000 a year), but it holds 7 `Tram` bookings of -205 and 24 `Road` ones of -205 (4,992 a year each), and 410 is two of them. The Depots tab of the overview lists 7 road maintenance buildings (three at Bromsgrove, one at Retford, one at East Retford, two at Todmorden), each with an upkeep of $10,000, so the 7 `Tram` halves are one per building, with a `Road` half each among the 24. The boathouse's maintenance building (from the Boathouse mod, `ingo_boathouse_asset`; $15,000, pool 2) is the -616 `Water` booking (15,000 a year), not split. **Observed**, one save. Of the 24 `Road` -205s, 7 are therefore the maintenance buildings' halves and 17 are something else (road stops of $5,000 are the likely rest; the Stations tab was cut off at the bottom, so the count was not checked).
- **The Depots tab shows the base, the window adds the accumulated part.** The tab listed both road depots at $30,000 (the window of one showed $30,503) and all seven maintenance buildings at $10,000 (two windows showed $10,759 and $11,242). The two `Road` bookings of -1,232 are the two road depots, the 2 x -4,106 `Water` bookings the two ship depots ($100,000 each), -2,053 twice the two warehouses ($50,000), -1,724 the Bromsgrove Station ($42,000, a `modular_terminal` with two specialized terminals), -862 twice the two `modular_terminal` cargo stations at East Retford and Todmorden ($21,000, one specialized terminal each; why 21,000 is in the next section), and -985 twice the ports ($24,000). Every row of this tab that was read matches a booking. The tab's Usage column is the pool use ("5 / 12"); the buildings that had vehicles were the road depots at Bromsgrove (5 of 12) and Todmorden (12 of 12) and five maintenance buildings (7, 3, 12, 4 and 6 of 16); the ship depot at Todmorden East had 6 of 8, the other ship depot and the boathouse none.
- **A building's accumulated cost is booked as its vehicle maintenance at the next batch, and then starts again. Confirmed** (604). The batch has one `Road` vehicle maintenance booking per road building that holds vehicles (7) and one `Water` (-3,510, the ship depot with ships); the buildings with none have no booking. The windows read at clock 5,997,000 showed accumulated parts of 503 (the Bromsgrove Road Depot), 759 and 1,242 (the two Todmorden maintenance buildings). The next save, 6,400 units later, held the batch at 6,000,000 with `vehicle maintenance` bookings of -503, -759 and -1,242 (and -666, -1,088, -1,122, -1,560 and -3,510), the same three figures to the unit. The batch before (5,940,000) had -438, -654, -693, -945, -1,036, -1,290 and -1,560, so the bookings change from batch to batch as the accumulated cost does. The accumulated figure in the window had not been growing in small steps toward the booking: it was already the full amount 3,000 units before it was booked. What adds to it (a repaired vehicle) was not tested; the player's reading is that vehicles passing through the building's catchment add to it. Which booking belongs to which building is read from the windows for three buildings and by size for the rest.

### A `modular_terminal` cargo station's upkeep: universal and specialized terminals (604)

**Observed** on 604 in the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game) (windows read on 25 April 1919, build menu read on the same day), with the numbers from the game files named below. A `modular_terminal` cargo station window showed $21,000/Year for a station with one terminal (East Retford, a bulk terminal; Todmorden Fish Station, a goods terminal) and $42,000/Year for the Bromsgrove Station with two bulk terminals. The build menu listed the Goods Cargo Platform (`cargo_platform_goods`) at $60,000 with $10,000/Year. So the window's figure is not the platform's: a terminal is more than one platform module.

- **The files give the sum, Confirmed as arithmetic (604).** In `stations/street.zip`, the cargo platform modules (`cargo_platform_bulk`, `_flatbed`, `_goods`, `_liquid`) each have a price of 60,000 and an upkeep of 10,000, the entrance module 6,000 and 1,000, and the universal `cargo_platform` module 30,000 and 5,000. The station template (`modular_terminal.con.lua`) states a new station's upkeep as two platforms plus one entrance: 2 x 10,000 + 1,000 = 21,000 for specialized platforms, 2 x 5,000 + 1,000 = 11,000 for universal ones, and its price as six times that figure (126,000 and 66,000). The windows' 21,000 is the first of these, and 42,000 is twice it.
- **Only the universal template is offered.** The calls that would add a bulk, flatbed, goods or liquid template are commented out in the same file, for all three eras. So a bulk or goods terminal is the universal station with its platforms swapped for specialized modules (label Bulk or Goods in the Terminals list), which doubles the platform part of the upkeep: 11,000 becomes 21,000. The 11,000 of an untouched universal station was not read from a window. **Open**: whether the swap is one module per side of the lane (two per terminal), which the sums imply; the module list of a saved station was not read.
- **Extra terminals.** The template's range for a `modular_terminal` station ends at the base plus three extra platform pairs of 2 x the platform figure. Bromsgrove's two terminals read 42,000, not 41,000 (base plus one pair of 20,000). The player had added a second road entrance to that station after making it a two-terminal one, which is the missing 1,000: four specialized platform modules (40,000) and two entrances (2,000). **Observed**, one station, the entrance count from the player's account and not read from the save; so an extra terminal alone adds 20,000, and each added entrance module 1,000.
- **Era factors.** The template multiplies its figures by 1 (1900 to 1949), 3 (from 1950) and 9 (from 1990); the modules carry no such factor. These are the file's values: no save from 1950 or later was read. The 1920 step of [inflation.md](inflation.md#the-year-cost-table) acts on the window's figure (31,500 for the one-terminal station, 1.5 x 21,000) and is a separate rule.
