# Maintenance buildings and depots: origin, facing, footprint and coverage

**Observed** on 604 in the Small subarctic game (save `1930`: eight maintenance buildings and two road depots; the footprint checked against the player's top-down screenshots of two maintenance buildings). Footprints, radii and pools are from the construction scripts in `base/content/depots/road.zip` (`road/road_maint_station.script.lua`, `road/road_depot/road_depot.script.lua`, `road/tram_depot/tram_depot.script.lua`).

- **One instance per building.** The models are `depots/road/road_maint_station/road_maint_station.mdl`, `.../road_depot/road_depot.mdl` and `.../tram_depot/tram_depot.mdl`. A road depot also has a `road_depot_sm` instance at the same point ([vehicles.md](vehicles.md) notes it), so count `road_depot` only. The translation is the construction's origin and the first two floats of the matrix (the "facing" that `tools/model_instances.py` prints) are the map image of the model's x axis. The model's y axis is that pair turned a quarter turn: for a facing (fx, fy) the point at local (a, b) lies at the origin plus a times (fx, fy) plus b times (-fy, fx).
- **The ground face is a rectangle in local coordinates, off centre from the origin.** Maintenance building 26.3 by 26.05 m (local x -12.5 to 13.8, y -15.0 to 11.05, centre about 2 m from the origin); road depot 56.2 by 40.0 m (x -17.7 to 38.5, y -20.1 to 19.9); tram depot 32.0 by 72.4 m (x -9.3 to 22.7, y -22.0 to 50.3). Drawn from the saved origin and facing, the maintenance face matched both screenshots (edges within about 5 degrees of the outlines, a corner touching the road at one building and a couple of metres clear at the other), and a road depot's entrance road ended at its face's edge. No tram depot was in the save, so that one is unchecked. Which way a building's front faces was not checked.
- **A ship depot stands out over the water past its ground face.** **Observed** on 604, one game (a temperate game, one top-down screenshot of one ship depot with the game's white outline round it). The ship depot's script (`water/water_depot.script.lua` in `base/content/depots/water.zip`) gives a ground face of 64.4 by 82 m (local x -27.3 to 37.1, y -21.5 to 60.5) and, in its `collider` curve, three boxes, two of which reach past it: x -21.4 to 28.3 and y -48.4 to 23.2, and x 28.3 to 38.6 and y -48.4 to 6.4. The boxes cover the part over the water. The outline of the face and the boxes together is 108 m along local y, with a 27 m notch on one long side where only the box ends (28.3 against 37.1) and a step at the far end, and it matched the screenshot's outline in length, in the notch and in its position along the side. The match is by eye on one depot. The road, tram and rail depots and the road maintenance building have one collider box equal to the face, so their face is the whole footprint. The ship maintenance building's collider also equals its face, yet its model reaches 32 m past it on the water side and the script adds a separate water-obstacle box there, so its water part is **Open**.
- **Each kind declares a catchment radius, a maintenance pool and the vehicle modes it serves.** The maintenance building: 600 m (the script's default; it can be overridden), pool 16, `BUS`, `TRUCK`, `TRAM`, `ELECTRIC_TRAM`. The road depot: 400 m, pool 12, `BUS`, `TRUCK`. The tram depot: 400 m, pool 12, `TRAM`, `ELECTRIC_TRAM`. So a depot has a pool for its own modes, and only the maintenance building serves buses, trucks and trams together. The other carriers have their own: rail depot 800 m, pool 6, and rail maintenance building 800 m, pool 12 (`TRAIN`, `ELECTRIC_TRAIN`); ship depot 1000 m, pool 8, and ship maintenance building 1400 m, pool 12; air: hangar modules of the airfield and airport 550 m, pool 16, and the heliport 800 m, pool 4. The 16 matches the "0/16" and "2/16" in the windows.
- **The modes are vehicle modes, not lane modes.** Street templates list `TRAM` and `ELECTRIC_TRAM` on tram lanes, and the rail track templates (`simple`, `standard`, `high_speed`, with catenary variants) list `TRAM_TRACK` and `ELECTRIC_TRAM_TRACK` beside `TRAIN`, so trams can run on rails. No depot script lists a `_TRACK` mode, so by the scripts a tram is served by mode and distance whichever it runs on. That the game really measures it so, and from the origin, is **Open**.
- **What decides which building a vehicle is listed under is Open; the radius holds from inside.** In save `1930` two trucks stood in reach of two maintenance buildings (origins measured from the model instances). Truck A was 504 m from the building at the larger x (the one drawn at the top in a `--north sun` picture, the north one by the sun's frame) and 411 m from the other, truck B 319 m from the first and over 1,200 m from the second. The first building's window listed both trucks and the second's listed none, so both are inside the 600 m circle and listed there (**Observed**, 604, one game, two vehicles), while truck A, inside the south building's 600 m circle too, was not listed under it, so a circle does not decide by itself which building lists a vehicle. The farthest vehicle listed there was at 504 m, so the circle's edge was not tested by vehicles.
- **The drawn circle's radius matches the script (604, Observed, a Tiny subarctic game, one picture of each, the game paused).** Selecting a building draws a pale ring on the ground round its origin. A maintenance building's ring was nearly top-down in the picture and read 598 to 603 m from three or four road nodes matched by hand (a similarity fit, residual about 10 px on a radius of about 467 px; a tilt of up to 10 or 15 degrees would move that by a few percent, the fit's scale along the road and across it agreeing within 4 percent). A road depot's ring, in a tilted picture cut by the screen's edge, read about 400 m near its origin and 340 to 480 m over the rest of the ring, because the hand fit drifts away from the matched nodes: **Open** whether it is exactly 400 m. The ring is centred on the building's origin (to within the fit, about 10 px). The building's window, in the same pictures, showed the pool (0 of 16 and 0 of 12 with no vehicle) and the upkeep per year ($60,000 and $180,000 at 1980 prices in a new game). [transit-overlay.md](transit-overlay.md#station-catchment) has the station catchment measured the same way.
- **A ship depot's ring is not always centred on its origin, and the offset is not a fixed local one (604, Observed, one temperate game, about 10 m error each).** Screenshots of a selected depot show its ring and a pale light column; the column's foot marks the ring's centre. Each shot was placed on the map with `tools/shot_place.py` (ships named in the picture, the game paused so the autosave of that date holds them where the picture does; [town-centre.md](town-centre.md) has the camera limits) and the foot mapped onto the ground. Huddersfield Ship Depot's foot lay within 2 m of its origin. Kavoj Ship Depot North's lay about 66 m from its origin (21 m east, 63 m north, on the water side; local +62 along the facing and -22 across it). The three side-by-side Brentwood depots, built alike and facing alike, had feet 29, 16 and 43 m from their origins in different directions (the first two toward the group's middle, the third not). So the earlier by-eye reading of 55 to 75 m along local -x for two depots does not hold as a rule. The save holds one position per construction (the `water_depot.con` record has the origin once, no second point within 500 m), so the offset is not stored as such; what the game centres the ring on is **Open** (the script's `snapPoint.waterPoints` of 40 m in local y does not fit any of these). A road depot and a ship maintenance building had the column on the building. `terrain_map.py --coverage` draws every ring at the origin and is off by the above for some ship depots. The Transit Overlay export has no depot or maintenance records, so it cannot measure this.
