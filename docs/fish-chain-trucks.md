# The fish chain after seven trucks replaced the carts

The cart-era fish chain of [fish-chain.md](fish-chain.md) (the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game)) read again after the player bought seven trucks for the road line (see [vehicle-sale-bookings.md](vehicle-sale-bookings.md)): stocks drain, a second road stop receives what the first does not take, and the warehouse runs empty. All 604, one game, so every label is **Observed** unless marked.

## In short

- **A truck line moved about 2.5 times what the cart line did (Observed):** 96 to 108 fish per 365,000 units against 32 to 40.
- **The warehouse stock fell while the trucks took more than arrived, and ran empty about 2.1 million units after the projection's start (Observed).** Trucks then left with what there was.
- **`itemsAtStop<n>_c6` is numbered by position on the line, one entry per vehicle arrival (Observed).** A stop 2 entry is what a truck still held when it reached the second stop, so the lists show the split between stops, not the reason.
- **The port's surplus (about 9% of the fish) goes to town buildings through three other destination keys, and buildings receive exactly what the destination keys count (Observed).** Which building owns a group is Open.
- **Destination key 25,505 counts arrivals at the first stop, not its unloads (Observed).**
- **Spoiled arrivals stopped, and `itemsLost6` stopped when the warehouse emptied** ([unsatisfied-lists.md](unsatisfied-lists.md#after-trucks-replaced-the-carts-604)).

## Evidence and tests

### The same chain after seven trucks replaced the carts: drained stocks, a second stop (604)

**Observed** on 604 in the same game, in two autosaves made after the player bought seven `benz1912_box` trucks for the road line (the purchase is in [vehicles.md](vehicles.md#road-vehicles-bought-an-instance-at-the-depot-at-once-604) and [vehicle-sale-bookings.md](vehicle-sale-bookings.md)): clock 45,001,200 (9 April 1914 on screen) and 46,198,800 (30 June 1915). The calendar ran at 2,000 clock units a day through the first stretch and at 16,000 in the last, so figures are given per clock unit. The production, loaded and unloaded lists keep every entry, so one late save gives the whole time series; the stop arrival lists do not (below). One game, so every label is **Observed**.

- **A truck line moves about 2.5 times what the cart line did.** The line's `itemsTransported6` list grew in steps of 4 to 16 with carts (32 to 40 fish per 365,000 units in the last year before the purchase) and in steps of 12, 24, 36 and 60 (27 and 33 once each) with the trucks (96 to 108 per 365,000). The buy window showed a capacity of 12 for the model, and every arrival at the first road stop is an entry of exactly 12; a step of 36 is three trucks stamped together. Arrivals come 30,000 to 50,000 units apart, which fits seven trucks on a cycle of about 250,000.
- **The warehouse stock fell once the trucks could take more than arrived.** Unloaded minus loaded read 500 (within 12) from 36.5 million to 43.1 million, 483 at 43.8 million, 380 at 45.0 million and 245 at 46.2 million, about 22 fewer per 200,000 units. The trucks loaded about 65 per 200,000 against about 39 arriving. At that rate the stock reaches zero about 2.2 million units after the second save; after that loads can only match arrivals. That is a projection, not read from a later save.
- **`itemsAtStop<n>_c6` is numbered by position on the line, one entry per vehicle arrival.** The truck line's group holds `itemsAtStop1_c6` (first road stop) and `itemsAtStop2_c6` (second road stop), with matching `itemsAtStopUnsatisfied<n>_c6` lists. Stop 1 entries are 12 each. A stop 2 entry is what a truck still held when it reached the second stop, so it is what the first stop did not take. The stop 2 list begins at clock 44,698,432 with an entry of 0 and its first fish are at 44,719,600. From then to 45.0 million nine trucks brought 108 fish to the first stop and 68 reached the second, so the first stop took about 40. From 45.0 million to 46.2 million, 31 trucks brought 372 and 323 reached the second stop: the first stop took about 13%, and the recent stop 2 entries are 11 or 12. Before 44.7 million the first stop took all 12 every time. This agrees with the player's reading that the first stop's demand has been met and the rest goes on; the lists show the split, not the reason.
- **The first stop's unloaded total plus the second stop's arrivals equals its arrivals, to about two loads.** At 46.2 million the first stop had 3,864 arrivals and 3,447 unloaded (417 apart) with 391 arrivals at the second stop; at 45.0 million the figures were 3,492, 3,401 and 68. The remainder (26 and 23) is the size of a couple of loads in transit. The unloaded list of the first stop is the one the sections above call the town stop's; its latest period bar read 35 against 272 in the one before (the period was 62% over).
- **The stop arrival lists keep only a recent window.** The first entry of `itemsAtStop1_c6` is a carried total (3,060 at 43,507,000 in the first save, 3,396 at 44,713,000 in the second, about 40 entries each), the older entries having been folded into it. The industries' and warehouse's lists show no such trimming (hundreds of entries from the first minutes). How the window is chosen (an entry count of about 40 here, about 280 for the `itemsAtDestination6` list of the same line) was not established.
- The fishing industries ran dry once the trucks took their output as fast as it appeared ([industries.md](industries.md#later-readings-the-fish-chain-before-and-after-the-trucks)), and spoiled arrivals stopped ([unsatisfied-lists.md](unsatisfied-lists.md#after-trucks-replaced-the-carts-604)).
- **The port still counts more than the warehouse takes, with room in the warehouse.** The port's unloaded total minus the warehouse's was 287 at 43,435,000, 307 at 45.0 million and 325 at 46.2 million, while the warehouse stood at 500, 380 and 245. Another 38 fish left the port's unloading without entering the warehouse when it was not full, which extends the 12 early cases [above](fish-chain.md#what-reaches-the-warehouse-and-what-does-not). Where they land: [below](#the-fish-chain-at-clock-546-million-an-empty-warehouse-overflow-fish-to-buildings-604).

### The fish chain at clock 54.6 million: an empty warehouse, overflow fish to buildings (604)

**Observed** in the autosave at clock 54,600,400 (6 December 1916 on screen, calendar 0.25x, 16,000 units a day), with totals at earlier clocks read from the same save. The interval compared is 46,198,800 (the last save of the section above) to 54,600,400.

- **The industries still ship all they make.** In the interval the three fishing industries produced 525, 402 and 680 and loaded 525, 408 and 670, and their stocks stayed at 0, 0 and 10 ([industries.md](industries.md#later-readings-the-fish-chain-before-and-after-the-trucks)). The port unloaded 1,617, the same fish.
- **The warehouse ran empty.** Its stock (unloaded minus loaded) read 257 at 46.2 million, 139 at 47.0 million, 11 at 48.0 million and 0 at 48.3 million, and 0 to 6 at every later reading. The projection above (zero about 2.2 million units after 46.2 million) was close: it took about 2.1 million.
- **Trucks now leave with what there is.** The last 20 arrivals at the first road stop carried 3 to 12 fish, 163 in all, about 8 per 12-fish truck. The player's Lines window at about the same time showed the line with 7 vehicles, capacity 84 and a load of 6.
- **The first stop takes all the fish again.** The second stop's unloaded total read 220, 484, 578 and 605 at 46.2, 48.0, 50.0 and 52.0 million, with its last entry at 52,076,600, and its arrival list has not grown since 51.84 million. The first stop unloaded 64, 249, 341 and 441 in the four stretches between those clocks and the save.
- **Destination key 25,505 counts arrivals, not unloads.** Its `itemsAtDestination6` total (5,568) equals the truck line's `itemsAtStop1_c6` total, the fish reaching the first stop, and not that stop's unloaded total (4,541). The 1,027 between them are the second stop's arrivals.
- **The port's surplus goes through the three other destination keys.** Port unloaded minus warehouse unloaded read 436, 445, 455 and 470 at clock 53.0, 53.4, 54.0 and 54.6 million. The `itemsAtDestination6` totals of keys 25,658, 33,616 and 33,639 rose by 0 + 1 + 8, 4 + 5 + 1 and 1 + 12 + 2 in the same three stretches: 9, 10 and 15, the same as the surplus. Over the whole interval the surplus was 145 of the 1,617 fish (9%), with the warehouse empty, so it is not overflow from a full warehouse. The destination lists start at 52.5 to 52.9 million (trimmed), so the match covers only the last 1.6 million units.
- **Town buildings receive exactly what the destination keys count.** From 53.13 to 54.60 million the 80 groups with `itemsUnloaded6` and `itemsConsumed6` (consumer buildings) gained 274 fish, in entries of 1 or 2, and the four destination keys gained 278 (247 at 25,505 and 31 at the other three). Every building entry lies within 6,000 units of a destination entry, typically 2,600 units before or after it, and none at a ship's unloading time, which is why the search at the port's time stamps [above](fish-chain.md#what-reaches-the-warehouse-and-what-does-not) found nothing. Giving each building entry the key of the nearest destination entry puts 30 fish on the port's keys (31 counted), in about ten buildings that took only or mostly those fish. The player identifies them as the commercial buildings near the port. The lists give neither a building's type nor its place, because which building owns a group is still unknown.
- No spoiled fish arrived in the interval, and the fishing industries' `itemsLost6` lists stopped when the warehouse emptied ([unsatisfied-lists.md](unsatisfied-lists.md#after-trucks-replaced-the-carts-604)).
