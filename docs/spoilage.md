# Spoiled cargo

How a save shows cargo that went bad on the way: the spoiled counters of the game's windows, the `itemsLost` statistics lists, and what a warehouse does with spoiled cargo. The "unsatisfied" lists at stops and edges are in [unsatisfied-lists.md](unsatisfied-lists.md). Where the spoiled count itself is kept is **Open**. Checked on 604 in one game, the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game) and its fish chain ([fish-chain.md](fish-chain.md#a-fish-chain-read-end-to-end-windows-groups-a-truck-queue-604)).

## In short

All of this is from one game on 604 (the Small subarctic game), so every label is for that game.

- **The game keeps spoilage per unit or per delivery batch, not as one number per stock (Observed; where it is stored is Open).** The interface asks the engine for a total, a bad count and an average quality for any vehicle, line, stop or stock (**Confirmed** in `gui/main/cargo_util.tl`, `base/content/gui.zip`). A warehouse that held 15 spoiled fish among about 450 handed carts only good fish, so it can tell them apart. No `spoil`, `quality`, `decay` or `countBad` string is in the decompressed stream, so the data is either inside the vehicle and stock records or derived from load times; neither was found.
- **A load goes bad by age against the cargo's expected delivery time (Observed for fish: 1,687,500 units, [delivery-times.md](delivery-times.md)).** The cargo bar in a ship's window may be the load's age as a share of that time (a fit within a few points, **Open**). The 15 spoiled fish a ship carried at 0% were old when loaded, probably from the fishing industry's stock (**Open**).
- **A warehouse does not discard spoiled units soon (Observed, one warehouse).** The 15 stayed in stock, counted in it, for 4.7 million units, 2.8 times the delivery time, with the warehouse nearly full. They were gone from its window later, in step with 15 single-fish entries at a town stop ([unsatisfied-lists.md](unsatisfied-lists.md)), which fits "the carts carried them out" but was not followed fish by fish (**Open**).
- **The `itemsAtStopUnsatisfied`, `itemsAtDestinationUnsatisfied` and `itemsAtEdgeUnsatisfied` lists count fish that reached a place spoiled or unusable (Observed; "spoiled" rather than "refused" is Open).** They grow one fish at a time as a cart unloads and stay still when fresh fish arrive. Detail in [unsatisfied-lists.md](unsatisfied-lists.md); the lists are in [Where spoilage shows in the save](#where-spoilage-shows-in-the-save).
- **A town building takes spoiled fish into its totals (Observed once).** Its unloaded and consumed lists rose by about what the stop delivered. A refusal was not tested; it needs one building's window before and after a delivery.
- **`itemsLost` is not settled.** For fish a lead says `itemsLost6` counts fish dropped for age and credited to the industry that made them ([unsatisfied-lists.md](unsatisfied-lists.md); **Open**). `itemsLost0` (passengers) grew by 14 entries in one stretch and what a lost passenger is was not tested (**Open**).
- **Not tested:** the player's report that a specialized warehouse stops the spoil timer and a universal one does not ([warehouses.md](warehouses.md)).

## Where spoilage shows in the save

| List | What it probably counts | Label |
|---|---|---|
| `itemsAtStopUnsatisfied1_c6` (the digit and the `_c` number as seen for fish; the digit's meaning is not settled) | fish that reached the stop spoiled or unusable; at the port, one entry of 15 at the time they were unloaded | Observed; meaning Open |
| `itemsAtDestinationUnsatisfied6` | the same per destination group, a subset of `itemsAtDestination6`, one entry per fish | Observed, one stop |
| `itemsAtEdgeUnsatisfied0_c6`, `itemsAtEdgeUnsatisfied1_c6` (one per direction) | the same along the route's edges, so spoiled fish can be placed along the road | Observed |
| `itemsLost6`, `itemsLost0` | fish (id 6) probably dropped for age; passengers (id 0) unknown | Open |

The cargo ids are positions in the save's cargo list ([cargo-ids.md](cargo-ids.md#the-id-is-the-position-in-the-cargo-list)). The lists sit in groups with their `itemsTransported`, `itemsConsumed` and `itemsUnloaded` neighbours, as other `items` lists do.

## Evidence and tests

### Spoiled cargo and `itemsLost` (604)

**Observed** on 604 in the Small subarctic game, save `1401` (clock 10,026,200, paused) with the player's screenshots from the two minutes before it, and `1339` (6,428,600) for comparison. **Open**: where the spoiled count is kept.

#### What the windows and the game's files show

- **What the game's windows show.** Ship 8 (a `sydfart` on a fishing line, stuck at the port, 0 km/h) read 15 of 20 fish aboard with a sad-face counter of 15 beside it (presumably the spoiled count, the interface's `numSpoiled`, so all 15 aboard); Ship 4 (a Canadian Trawler unloading at the same terminal) read 3 of 10 fish and no counter. The port had one terminal of type Passenger and Cargo with all three fishing lines on it. A cart on the road line (`horsewagon_1850_v2` and `horsewagon_1850_usa_v2`) read 4 of 4 and the line 16 of 28, with no counter. The fish in the warehouse read 445 of 500.
- **What the game's files say.** The user interface reads a spoiled count (`countBad`) and an average quality for a vehicle, a line, a stop or a stock from engine calls (`gui/main/cargo_util.tl` in `base/content/gui.zip`, `numSpoiled` and `spoiledCount` in `base/tealdef/gui/main/cargo_util.d.tl`). The fish definition (`cargos/fish/fish.cargo.lua` in `base/content/cargos/fish.zip`) holds a delivery time of 1,125 seconds and decay values that its price function (`constantFn`) does not use. How the engine decides a unit is bad was not read from a script.
- **Not in the stream by name.** The decompressed `1401` stream held no `spoil`, `quality`, `decay` or `countBad` string, and there is no `itemsLost6` list (6 is fish, see [cargo-ids.md](cargo-ids.md)). So spoilage is either per-unit data inside the vehicle and stock records or a number derived from times, and neither was found.

#### The spoiled fish stay in the warehouse stock

- **Spoiled fish showed up in the warehouse stock right after unloading.** About 12 minutes after `1401` the player saw Ship 8 docked at the port ("Unloading - Speed: 200%", the same 200% Ship 4 showed): it held 1 of 20 fish with a spoiled counter of 1, down from 15 of 20 and 15 spoiled in the screenshots before `1401` and in the one just before `1410` (18 km/h, turning around). The Todmorden Warehouse, which read 445 of 500 fish with no counter at 14:00, read 455 of 500 with a counter of 14 (the same sad face, in yellow). So at the time of the screenshots the 14 spoiled fish that left the ship were in the warehouse, counted in the stock, and the counter is the spoiled share of the stock. The player's suspicion is that the warehouse discards spoiled units after taking them in, which these screenshots would not show, since they were taken within moments of the unloading. The next save, `1413` (14:13:50, clock 10,202,200, 20,000 units after `1412`), and its screenshots settled the unloading: Ship 8 read 0 of 20 and was leaving for its fishing grounds at 10 km/h, and the warehouse read 456 of 500 with a counter of 15. That is the 14 plus the ship's last spoiled fish, so all 15 spoiled fish of Ship 8 were in the warehouse stock when unloading ended, and none had been discarded. Only 20 s of game clock separated the two saves, so this does not rule out a later discard. Then a cart of the road line (`horsewagon_1850_v2` and `horsewagon_1850_usa_v2`) loaded fish at the warehouse (save `1414`, 14:15:18, clock 10,236,600, 34,400 units after `1413`, with screenshots at 14:15:02 to 14:15:45): the warehouse read 452 of 500 (four fewer, the cart's capacity) with the counter still at 15, and the cart (Road Vehicle 4) read 4 of 4 fish and no counter. So the cart took four good fish and left the spoiled ones in the stock, and the warehouse had still not discarded them 34,400 units (about 34 s at 1x) after the last of them arrived. `itemsLost0` was unchanged in `1414` too. A ship of the other fishing line, Ship 1 (a Canadian Trawler, 10 of 10 fish, no counter), was docking at the port at 5 km/h in the last screenshot. Not tested: the warehouse window read again after minutes of game time, with a save to compare its `items` lists, would show whether the 15 stay. The save made 20 s after those screenshots, `1412` (clock 10,182,200, paused), has the same `itemsLost0` list as `1401` and `1410` (last entry 17) and the same five stuck-vehicle notifications, so unloading the spoiled fish added to neither (the five lost items of `1401` stay the only ones). **Observed**, one ship; where the counter is stored is still **Open**.
- **Spoilage is tracked per unit or per delivery batch, not as one number** (the player's inference, supported by the screenshots; **Open** where). In the screenshots of `1414` and of `1415` (14:19:16, clock 10,508,600) every cart that took fish out of the warehouse held 4 of 4 with no spoiled counter (Road Vehicles 4, 6 and 2), while the warehouse kept a counter of 15 among about 450 fish. If the warehouse kept only a total and a spoiled count, a cart taking 4 of 450 would be expected to take some spoiled ones now and then; it took none, so the game can tell which fish are which. The player's second argument is that the warehouse's average quality goes down as staler fish are delivered, which would also need the age of each arrival (not yet checked against a screenshot of the average). The game's own interface code reads three numbers for any stock, vehicle or line, `countTotal`, `countBad` and `averageQuality`, from engine calls (`api.engine.util.cargo.getCargoQualityDataFor...` in `gui/main/cargo_util.tl`), which fits per-unit or per-batch data behind them. The save was not searched for it: a time and a count per arrival in the warehouse's stock record are the shape to look for, and `itemsLost0` stayed at 17 in `1415` as well.
- **Save `1427` (14:27:48, clock 11,239,800, paused) after about 1.06 million more units: the 15 spoiled fish are still in the warehouse.** The player read the warehouse at 493 of 500 with the counter still at 15. The spoiled fish arrived at about 10,180,000, so the warehouse had kept them for about 1.06 million units, 63% of the fish expected delivery time of 1,687,500, and it was close to full. The player's suspicion that a warehouse discards spoiled units is not supported in that time; it is not ruled out for a longer one. The full-warehouse test of the observation above is now within reach: 7 more fish.
- **Specialized and universal warehouses (player's observation, not read from the save).** The player reports that in a specialized warehouse the spoil timer of the cargo stops, and in a universal warehouse it keeps running. Not tested here; the warehouse modules are in [warehouses.md](warehouses.md).

#### A load's age and the window's percentage

- **Where a ship's load instant is in the save, and what the window's percentage may be** (**Open**, one game, save `1416` at clock 10,720,600). The `itemsLoaded6` lists of the fishing industries record every load as one entry (time, running total). One list had entries of exactly 20 each (a `sydfart` fills 20) at 9,213,000, 9,831,800, 9,907,400, 10,130,800, 10,428,200 and 10,625,600, another entries of 10 (a `british_columbia` fills 10) at 9,451,600, 9,653,600, 10,267,200 and 10,514,800 with a 20 at 10,002,600. The cargo bar in a moving ship's window (a box with a clock, then a percentage; the ships at sea read 37, 47, 52, 57, 65 and 73%, Ship 8 stuck at the port 0%, Ship 4 unloading 35%) may be the age of the load as a share of the expected delivery time (1,687,500 units for fish, [delivery-times.md](delivery-times.md)): the ages of the six most recent 20-fish loads are 5.6, 17.3, 35.0, 48.2, 52.7 and 89% at that clock, and the readings sit within a few points of these where a load exists to match (35.0 against 37, 48.2 against 47, 52.7 against 52, 63.3 against 65, 75.3 against 73). The screenshots were taken up to half a minute apart on a game that may have been running, so this is a fit, not a match, and which ship took which load was not identified. It does not explain Ship 8: its 15 spoiled fish were aboard at 0%, and a load of 20 made at 9,831,800 or 9,907,400 was under 1,687,500 units old when they were seen at about 10,000,000. So the spoiled fish were old when they were loaded, probably from the industry's stock (which starts full and loses its oldest fish first, if it works that way); a screenshot of the fishing industry's window, with its stock and spoiled counter, would test that.

#### The `itemsLost` lists

- **`itemsLost0` grew.** In `1339` the list (cargo id 0, passengers by the save's cargo list, [cargo-ids.md](cargo-ids.md#the-id-is-the-position-in-the-cargo-list)) held (4,017,749, 0) and (4,137,800, 3). In `1401` it also held (9,715,200, 5), (9,852,800, 14) and (9,957,000, 17): 14 more lost items, all after the third autosave of the day (clock 9,046,200) and 104,200 to 137,600 units apart. Id 0 is passengers, not fish, so these are probably not the spoiled fish; what a lost passenger is was not tested (**Open**). The list appears in several copies of one group, as the other `items` lists do.
- **`itemsLost6` lists exist in `1427` and did not in `1416`** (**Observed**, one game, **Open** what they count). Four lists, each started on a month boundary (10,835,749 and 10,957,499, which are 89 and 90 months of 121,750 units minus one) and each in a group beside `itemsTransported6` lists (the pattern of a line or station group, not an industry's): (10,894,000, 2) then (11,003,000, 14); (10,894,000, 1) then (11,002,200, 5); (10,893,200, 1) then (11,001,400, 6); and (11,003,000, 3). The first entries (10,893,200 and 10,894,000) lie 6,500 to 7,300 units before 10,900,500, the time a fish loaded at 9,213,000 (a 20-fish load in the lists above) passes the 1,687,500 limit, which is a rough fit for "fish that went bad on the way"; the three latest entries lie within 1,600 units of 11,002,000, and no load at about 9,314,000 (11,002,000 minus 1,687,500) was found in the load lists read, so that part is unexplained. A second group of `itemsLost` and `itemsLost0` lists, with entries (6, 8, 10) ending at 10,868,400, is also new, next to the old one that stayed at 17 (last entry 9,957,000). The count of stuck-vehicle notifications was unchanged. Which entities own these lists was not identified.

#### Town buildings take the fish

- **Town buildings take the fish, with a satisfaction figure.** The window of a commercial building in the town (screenshots at 14:27 to 14:28) read a fish input stock of 2 of 2 and a tinned-food one of 0 of 1, customers 6 of 6, a first row of 1 (the building's level), fish 100% and tinned food 0%, and 1900; a second building read 1 of 1 fish, 1 of 1 customers and fish 83%. The percentages look like how fully each cargo is supplied. The `itemsConsumed6` and `itemsUnloaded6` lists of the buildings (one list per building, started at the building's creation, with new ones at 11,079,249 for three buildings built in the 91st month) gained entries from 10,745,000 to 11,238,800; those deliveries come from the road line's carts (`horsewagon_1850_v2` and `horsewagon_1850_usa_v2`) out of the warehouse, so they do not tell which way ships unload.
