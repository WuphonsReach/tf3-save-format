# Entity names: what renames, new buildings and sales do to the name table

How the name table of [entity-names.md](entity-names.md) changes in play: a rename replaces the string in place, new entities append or take a freed slot, vehicle numbers count per kind, and people, stops and industries hold one or two slots. Checked on 604 in one new game and the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game).

## In short

- **A rename replaces the string in the slot it was in, for towns, lines, industries, stops and people (Observed, five tests).** Nothing else in the stream stores the name, and a name built from another name keeps no link back.
- **New entities mostly append, but one can take a freed slot (Observed, once).** Sold vehicles leave their name until a new vehicle takes the slot, so the table can hold a stale name and a name is not unique.
- **Default vehicle numbers count per kind and follow creation order, not table position (Observed);** after a sale the new ones take freed low numbers.
- **An industry or a stop sits in two adjacent slots with one name, a port in four (Observed);** the second slot is probably its station.
- **People are created in blocks of six consecutive slots per household (Observed, one residential building),** which would group people by home without a link; whether it holds everywhere is untested.
- **New lines come at the end of the table, line first, followed by their vehicles (Observed).**
- **A stream's size says nothing about a change:** set reordering before the table swamps a rename.

## Evidence and tests

### What a rename and new building do to the table (604)

**Observed** on 604, one new game, two saves 5,600 units of play and many edits apart (the tables had 1,302 and 1,350 strings, both found with the anchor method above, 1,297 strings before the anchor in each).

- **A rename replaces the string in place.** The player renamed a town (the second string in the table changed from `East Retford` to `Retford`) and two lines (`Line 1` and `Line 2` became `TOD Fish Cargo Truck 1` and `TOD Fishery Boats`), and the string at the same position changed each time. Nothing else moved. Stations built before the town rename kept `East Retford Station` and `East Retford Maintenance Building`, so a name that was built from another name is a plain string afterwards, with no link back.
- **New entities mostly append, but they can take a free slot.** 48 strings were added at the end (the new quarry, depots, warehouse, stations, vehicles, lines and a few people). Seven positions in the middle of the table, which held names of private people in the first save (and one `no name`), held new things in the second: stops (`George Street` twice, `Highfield Road` twice), road vehicles (`Road Vehicle 17`, `18`, `19`) and a line (`RET-TOD Bus 1`). So an entity that is deleted frees its slot, and a later entity can reuse it, which fits "position is tied to the id" in [entity-names.md](entity-names.md#layout). People are created and removed during play, so the people's slots are the ones reused. **Observed**, once.
- **The default vehicle numbers count per kind, whatever the slot.** The new road vehicles were `Road Vehicle 4` to `Road Vehicle 32` and the new ships `Ship 5` and `Ship 6`, matching 29 road vehicle purchases (4 at one time, 25 at another) and 2 ship purchases in the journal ([finances.md](finances.md#a-new-games-first-batch-of-bookings-while-paused)). Names shown as `Road Vehicle 17` to `19` sat in reused slots, while 20 to 32 were appended, so the number follows creation order and not table position.
- **Sold vehicles leave their name behind until a new vehicle takes the slot, and the new ones take the lowest free numbers (604, Observed, one game).** All seven road vehicles of one line (`Road Vehicle 1` to `7`, in seven neighbouring slots, count in the header 76 to 69) were sold and the save kept 76 `Road Vehicle` strings. Five road vehicles were then bought and the game saved paused at once: five of the seven slots held new text and the count was still 76, so no string was appended. The new names were `Road Vehicle 1` to `5`, in slots that had held 1, 2, 4, 5 and 7, in the order 5, 4, 1, 2, 3 (slots one to a few places apart, not the lowest free ones). The slots of `3` and `6` were not reused, so `Road Vehicle 3` appeared twice, once for a live vehicle and once as the leftover of a sold one. So the table can hold a stale name, and a name is not unique. With no sales the numbers still run in purchase order, as in the previous bullet; here they went back to the freed low numbers instead of continuing at 77. The game's tooltip showed the new vehicle as `Road Vehicle 1`. A new vehicle therefore has its name slot, its count in the header ([header.md](header.md#counts-the-load-dialog-shows)) and its model instance ([vehicles.md](vehicles.md#road-vehicles-bought-an-instance-at-the-depot-at-once-604)) at the moment of purchase.
- Line names the player chose (`RET-TOD Bus 1`, and the trucks' lines) are plain strings of the same table.

### Renaming industries, and a new line with its ships (604)

**Observed** on 604, the Small subarctic game: save `1246` against `1312` (made right after), with the names table parsed in both (1,849 names, then 1,864).

- **A rename is in place for every entity involved.** The player renamed the four fishing industries near one town (the original Fishing Grounds and the East, South and West ones) to "Todmorden Fishing 1" to "4" and the fishing line "TOD Fishery Boats" to "TOD Fishery 1". Each changed in the slot it was in. An industry's name sat in two slots: the original at slots 7 and 852 (far apart), the three that appeared in play in two adjacent slots each (1,357 and 1,358 for West, 1,391 and 1,392 for South, 1,625 and 1,626 for East). The second slot of each is probably the industry's station, whose stop name in the line window follows the industry's. **Observed**.
- **New lines and their vehicles are appended at the end of the table, line first.** Two new lines ("TOD Fishery 2", "TOD Fishery 3") came after the last entry, each followed by its ships (Ship 7 to 11, then Ship 12 to 19). The ships are numbered on from the last one, (numbers count per kind, see [above](#what-a-rename-and-new-building-do-to-the-table-604)). No other entity was added: the new lines' stops reuse the port's stop.

### Renaming people in place (604)

**Observed** on 604, the Small subarctic game: save `1445` against `1446` and `1447` (the same clock, 13,466,600, so the game had not moved on), with the names table parsed in each (2,374 names).

- **A person's name is one string in one slot, and a rename replaces it there.** The player renamed one passenger of a bus (`1446`) and then four more (`1447`), by adding a suffix. Each changed in the slot it was in; the table kept 2,374 entries, no slot was freed or added, and the name did not occur anywhere else in the stream. So no other structure stores a person's name.
- **Two people with one name are two slots, and a rename tells them apart.** One of the renamed riders had a namesake: the same name sat in slots 151 and 2,358, and the renamed text appeared in 151. A name alone does not identify a person ([lines.md](trip-plans.md#trip-plans-line-stops-and-vehicle-or-a-building-604)); a rename marks the slot of the one the player means.
- **The window keeps its order after a rename.** A vehicle's passenger list showed the same order before and after the four renames, so it is not sorted by name, and it is not in name slot order either (the slots read 1662, 151, 327, 519, 1862 and one of 81 or 917).
- **New people append.** Between `1447` and a later save made 79,400 units on, three strings were added at the end (two for a new industry and one person), and no slot changed text. A person who had left the bus did not lose a slot. Whether a person who disappears from the map frees a slot was not seen here (the entry above on freed slots stands).
- **The stream size is not a guide to what changed.** `1447` was 304 bytes longer than `1446`, against 28 bytes of added name text; the rest was set reordering before the table, which a byte diff cannot separate from real changes. Two autosaves of an unchanged game also differed by hundreds of bytes ([lines.md](trip-plans.md#trip-plans-line-stops-and-vehicle-or-a-building-604) uses the same-clock pairs only).
- **A second rename test, two walkers (`1457` against `1456`, both clock 13,992,600).** The player renamed two people who were walking along a street with no line in their route (their windows read Work to Home on foot). Slots 234 and 717 changed in place; the table kept its 2,398 entries, each new string occurred once in the stream, and the 458-record trip array ([lines.md](trip-plans.md#trip-plans-line-stops-and-vehicle-or-a-building-604)) was identical in both saves. So a rename touches only its string, whether the person is aboard a vehicle or on foot. The two decompressed streams differed by about 965,000 bytes in length (92.4 and 91.4 million) at the same clock, so a stream size says nothing about a change. **Observed**, one game.
- **A household sits in consecutive name slots (604, Observed, one residential building).** The window of the home of one renamed walker listed 12 residents (12 of 12). Their name slots were two runs of six consecutive numbers (232 to 237 and 576 to 581), none missing, and the window listed them in neither slot order nor run order. A shop's customer list (8 of 8) and an industrial building's worker list (6 of 6) held slots with no such runs (for the six workers 71, 234, 263, 534, 1,773 and 1,975). So people are created in blocks of six per household, and a block of consecutive slots marks the people who live together, which would give a way to group people by home without a link. Whether the six per block holds in every building, and what pairs two blocks in one building, was not tested.
- **A stop sits in two adjacent slots with one name (604, Observed, two stops of a road line).** The player renamed the line's two stops (`1658` against `1657`: "Park Road" to "Park Road-327", "Victoria Road" to "Victoria Road-1"). Slots 1,284 and 1,285 changed together, and 1,286 and 1,287 together; nothing else in the table changed, and no `items` group changed key (the groups of [fish-chain.md](fish-chain.md#a-fish-chain-read-end-to-end-windows-groups-a-truck-queue-604) kept theirs). The fishing industries already showed pairs (above), and a port sat in four slots (two names, two slots each), so a name is likely held once for the entity and once for its station. The slot is no help in finding the keys of the `items` groups: the slots of three lines and the keys of their `itemsTransported6` groups have no common step.
