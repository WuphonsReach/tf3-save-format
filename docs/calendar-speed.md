# Calendar speed in play

What the calendar speed setting does over a game: what one save tells about it, the Paused setting, a calendar started again after a long stop, a speed change while the game runs, and how the game's windows show the speed. The field itself, its values and the day table it drives are in [calendar.md](calendar.md#the-calendar-speed-field); the clock's rate at each play speed is in [calendar.md](calendar.md#the-game-clock). Checked on 604; each bullet names its game or catalog saves.

## Speed changes and stops

- **It is the setting at save time, not a history.** Players mostly leave the speed alone but some change it over time (user report, not measured). Reasons given: to stay in a year whose vehicles they like, since availability and other date-based unlocks follow the date, and to hold back costs that rise in later years. Neither reason was checked here. The game's scripts do raise construction costs with the date, in 20-year steps ([inflation.md](inflation.md#the-year-cost-table)); an in-game test of it is still **Open**. The field alone cannot say how long a game ran at which speed; the [day table](calendar.md#the-day-table) can. Catalog save 6428935 shows the effect: its shown date and its clock at plain 1.00x disagree by a factor of 0.88 although its field holds a single value (see [below](#calendar-speed-in-the-games-windows)). 70 of the 120 catalog saves hold 4000, but only 50 have a day table at 4000 ticks a day from start to end ([versions.md](versions.md#calendar-speed-in-the-catalog-2026-10-09)), so the setting is changed often enough to matter and a save at 4000 may have been run at other speeds earlier. **Observed**.
- **Paused (0).** The date stops and stays on the same day while the clock goes on counting (**Observed** in the game, one game: at the Paused setting the date shown stays where it was, and costs are tracked on the clock, not the date). The game's manual says calendar speed only changes when vehicles and infrastructure become available, and that cargo produced and consumed and vehicle maintenance are adapted to stay consistent ([Simulation overview](https://wiki.transportfever3.com/doku.php?id=gamemanual:simulation:overview), game manual), which fits. Whether the reporting window then shows the same period over and over was not checked in the stream. **Open**. The Paused setting and a paused game are separate: the same field holds 0 in saves that were running at 4x (catalog save 6422263, **Observed**).
- **Starting the calendar again after a long stop (604, Observed, the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game), saves `1704` and `1705`).** With the date stopped for 17.2 million ticks the day table had one entry (1 January 1900, began at tick 0, in every save from `1703` to `1704`). After the player set 0.25x and let it run for a moment, the next save (`1705`, paused, `millisPerDay` 16000, clock 17,270,200) had two entries: 2 January 1900 began at tick 17,264,000, 6,200 ticks before the clock. A day at 0.25x is 16,000 ticks, so the calendar was set running at about tick 17,248,000, between the two saves. The header date was Julian day 2,415,022, the day table's last day and the date the player read on screen. So a game that was never run on the calendar keeps its first day for as long as the date is stopped, and the table grows from the tick the calendar starts. At `1706` (paused, clock 17,719,800) the table had 30 entries, the last 30 January 1900 beginning at tick 17,712,000 (7,800 ticks before the clock), and the screen showed Jan 30, 1900: 28 days after 2 January in 449,600 ticks, 16,000 per day at 0.25x. At `1708` (clock 18,891,800) the table had 103 entries, 13 April 1900 beginning at tick 18,880,000; 1707's 9 February began at 17,872,000, so 63 days took exactly 1,008,000 ticks.
- **A speed change made while running shows only as a change of day length (604, Observed, one game, five autosaves after save `1711`).** The player took the calendar from 0.25x to 0.50x with the game running at play speed 4 and did not say when. The autosaves held `millisPerDay` 16000 at 22,943,000 (22 December 1900) and 8000 at 24,143,000 (8 May 1901). The day table of the later one had every gap at 16,000 up to 4 January 1901, which began at tick 23,136,000, and every gap at 8,000 from 5 January on, at 23,152,000; no other gap length occurred. The change therefore falls in 4 January's 16,000 ticks, because the length of a day is fixed when the day starts (see the next paragraph for the test). At the clock's rate of about 4,000 units per real second at play speed 4 (see [calendar.md](calendar.md#the-game-clock)) that is 48 to 52 seconds after the first autosave's file time, and the clock reached 24,928,000, the start of 15 August 1901 in the table, about where the file time of a screenshot taken on that date put it. The game keeps no list of speed settings: the table's gaps are the history, found in the calendar speed field and the day table only (the rest of the stream was not searched for another copy). The table is one entry per day, so a change cannot be placed inside a day, and two changes inside one day might leave no trace (not tested). The statistics periods are no help: they stay on 1,461,000 clock units whatever the speed, see [statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md), so only their date labels move with the table. **A change does not cut the day under way** (604, Observed, one game, saves `1712` to `1713` and the autosave after them). Saves `1712` (0.50x) and `1713` (1.00x) were made paused 21 seconds apart at the same clock, 26,751,000, 7,000 ticks into 30 March 1902; the only byte that differed in the speed field was `millisPerDay`, 8000 to 4000, and the day table, `t1`, `t2` and the header were identical. After the game ran on at 1.00x, the autosave's table shows 30 March, begun at tick 26,744,000, with its full 8,000 ticks, then every later day at 4,000 from 31 March at tick 26,752,000. So a new speed applies from the next day boundary, and the day under way keeps the length it started with, even when the change comes 7,000 ticks in; the table cannot place a change more finely than that day. The same held for a change to a longer day. The player went back from 1.00x to 0.25x and named 29 May 1903 as the date beforehand; the table of the autosave after it has 28 May 1903 at 4,000 ticks (begun at tick 28,444,000) and 29 May at 16,000 (begun at 28,448,000), and every later day at 16,000. Across the whole game the table holds four runs of day length, 16,000, 8,000, 4,000 and 16,000, matching the player's three changes (the first, to 0.50x, was dated from the table alone).

## Calendar speed in the game's windows

- **Not a Lua key or a game setting.** The calendar speed (the 1.00x slider of the Weather and Time window) is not a key in any script state, and not in the game's `settings.lua` either (604; the file holds graphics, audio, unit and camera settings plus the remembered new-game choices). It is the `millisPerDay` of the `GAME_SPEED` engine component. Components are written without their names: one 604 stream had no `GameSpeed`, `millisPerDay` or `speedup` strings at all.
- **The Paused setting in the window.** In catalog save 6425796 (604) the Calendar Speed row read **Paused**, with the slider at its left end, while the game was paused and showed 18 February 1937; saves of the game played into 2061 read 0.50x and so on in that row while paused. That is `millisPerDay` 0, a setting of its own ([calendar.md](calendar.md#the-calendar-speed-field)). The newest stored timestamp there was 94,328,800 (26 July 1964 at plain 1.00x) against 54,248,000 for the date shown at plain 1.00x, a ratio of 0.575, which the [day table](calendar.md#the-day-table) explains: mixed speeds and a long stop. **Observed**.
- **Restored from another player's save.** Catalog save 6428935 (604) showed 0.50x in the Weather and Time window after loading, and the game came up running at its 2x play speed (the author saved it unpaused). A copy re-saved paused from that game, reloaded, came up paused and showed 0.50x again (**Observed**, one round trip). The loan offers fit the same speed, see [company.md](company.md#loans). Its shown date, 14 February 2191 from a 1960 start, is 84,416 days from 1 January 1960, while the newest stored timestamp is 95,878 days at plain 1.00x. The ratio is 0.88, which fits a game played at several speeds and so says nothing about the speed at save time.
