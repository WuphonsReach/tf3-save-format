# The cement chain read through the statistics lists

The cement chain of the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game), from a quarry to the town buildings, read with the `items*` lists of [statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md) as the [fish chain](fish-chain.md) was (how to read a list's total at an earlier clock is [there](fish-chain.md#the-fish-chain-read-through-the-statistics-lists)). 604, one game, so every label is **Observed** unless marked.

## Quarry, plant, warehouse, town (604)

**Observed** in the autosave at clock 54,600,400 (6 December 1916 on screen, calendar 0.25x). Stone is cargo 24 and cement 15 in this save ([cargo-ids.md](cargo-ids.md)). Two road lines carry the chain. The first runs quarry, cement plant, warehouse: it brings stone to the plant and takes the plant's cement on to the warehouse. The second runs from the warehouse to a stop in Retford. The player's Lines window from about the same time showed the first line with 9 vehicles of two models (capacity 36 in all) and the second with 23 trucks (capacity 138). The player had not changed either line since the first months of the game.

| Point | Group (lists) | Total at the save |
|---|---|---|
| Quarry | `itemsProduced24`, `itemsDestroyed24`, `itemsLoaded24` | 5,343, 3,475, 1,872 |
| First line | `itemsTransported24`, `itemsAtStop1_c24`, `itemsTransported15`, `itemsAtStop2_c15` | 1,912 stone, 926 cement |
| Plant's stop | `itemsUnloaded24`, `itemsLoaded15` | 1,852, 926 |
| Cement plant | `itemsUnloaded24` = `itemsConsumed24`, `itemsProduced15` = `itemsLoaded15` | 1,852, 926 |
| Warehouse | `itemsUnloaded15`, `itemsLoaded15` (two groups with the same two totals) | 922, 912 |
| Second line | `itemsTransported15`, `itemsAtStop1_c15` | 912, 882 |
| Town stop | `itemsUnloaded15`; destination key 28,561 (`itemsAtDestination15`) | 882; 882 |
| Consumer buildings | 23 groups with `itemsUnloaded15` and `itemsConsumed15` | 636 |

- **The plant turns 2 stone into 1 cement and keeps neither** (1,852 consumed for 926 made, all of it loaded). That is the recipe in [industry-chains.md](industry-chains.md). The first line's stone total is 40 above the quarry's because of an early `itemsLoaded24` of 40 at the plant's stop (clock 5.6 to 8.6 million).
- **The first line carries 4 stone a trip and is the bottleneck.** Every stone arrival at the plant is an entry of 4, and the cement arrivals at the warehouse are entries of 2, the cement those 4 stone make, carried on by the same vehicle. The quarry is the Bromsgrove Quarry of [industries.md](industries.md#a-quarrys-destroyed-stone-pulse-by-pulse-and-industries-that-start-empty-604); it sits at its cap and destroyed 580 of the 880 stone it made between clock 46.2 and 54.6 million .
- **What the lists put in transit equals the Lines window's Load.** The second line: 912 loaded less 882 arrived = 30, and the window's Load read 30. The first line: 20 stone (1,872 loaded less 1,852 unloaded) plus 4 cement (926 less 922) = 24, against a Load of 23.
- **The second line's vehicles run bunched.** Its loads come in windows of 1.7 to 1.9 million units, 13 to 16 loads each, and between windows nothing loads for 1.5 to 2.6 million units. Windows begin every 3,416,200 units on average (3,454,000 in the last two cycles), and the gap has shortened every cycle (2.64 million in the first, 1.54 million in the last). In each window the first loads are 6 (a truck's capacity), taken from the stock gathered since the last visit, and the rest are 2, one per delivery from the first line. The warehouse never held more than about 26. The pattern is the line's vehicles arriving as one bunch. That 13 to 16 loads come from 23 trucks suggests the others leave with nothing (an empty departure would add no entry), which is **Open**. A trip from the warehouse to the town takes about 1.7 million units: the loads of 2 made from 51.65 to 52.34 million arrived from 53.37 to 54.08 million.
- **The edge totals show the bunch on the road.** The second line has about 140 `itemsAtEdge*_c15` groups, one run of the stream. At the save their totals step 912, 906, 900, 894, 888 and then 882 for the rest, so five trucks of 6 were on the road, and 882 is what the town stop has received. Within each run of equal totals, the last times rise toward the run's town end, and the newest of each run (54.59 to 54.60 million) is where a truck stood at the save. The long 882 run's last times fall from 54.06 million at the town end to 53.09 million at its other end, about 11,700 units per edge group (84 groups), which is the last truck before the bunch. So in this stream the groups of one direction lie in route order, town end first, and the last times trace a vehicle's passage. **Observed**, one line in one save. It is a way to tell which edge group is where, which [the fish queue](fish-chain.md#a-stuck-vehicle-the-totals-along-the-route-show-the-queue) could not.
- **The small keyed `itemsTransported15` groups are the consumer buildings' tallies.** 23 groups hold only an `itemsTransported15` list under a 5-digit key, and their 23 totals (1 to 89) equal the 23 consumer buildings' `itemsUnloaded15` totals one for one. The key is probably the building's id. That was not checked against the [town building records](town-buildings.md).
- **The buildings' lists hold 246 fewer cement than the town stop unloaded.** The difference read 52, 144, 206, 240 and 246 at clock 10, 20, 30, 40 and 46.2 million, and 246 at every later reading. It grew while the town was young and then stopped. Whether buildings that were replaced took their lists with them was not checked (**Open**). Later saves read it differently ([below](#town-buildings-in-the-later-saves)).

## Replacing both lines' vehicles (604)

**Observed** in the same game from August 1917 to May 1918 (clock 58.4 to 63.2 million, calendar 0.25x), in three manual saves and two autosaves, with the player's screenshots of the line, depot and buy windows up to July 1918. The player replaced the stone line's nine carts (`horsewagon_1850_usa_v2` and `horse_cart_small`) with seven Benz 3-Ton Bulk trucks (capacity 12), then the delivery line's 23 trucks with 14 and later 27 of the same model. The bookings of the purchases and sales are in [finances.md](finances.md#selling-a-vehicle-is-a-positive-booking-in-the-purchase-category-604), and what the line windows showed in [lines.md](lines.md#the-lines-windows-frequency-rate-and-load-columns-604).

**The stone line.**

- **No stone reached the plant for about a month.** The last cart delivery of 4 was at 58,438,000 and the first truck delivery of 12 at 58,981,000, 0.55 million units after the purchase. Every arrival since is an entry of 12. The quarry's loaded list merges trucks that load close together (entries of 24 and 26), so count trucks from the plant's arrival list.
- **The quarry stopped destroying stone.** In the million units before the purchase it made 99, loaded 32 and destroyed 66. From 58.4 to 59.7 million it made 132, loaded 149 and destroyed 18, all in the month without deliveries; from 59.7 to 60.8 million it made 110, loaded 133 and destroyed nothing, and its stock fell from 400 to 342. Cement made rose from 18 per million units to about 40.
- **A vehicle's loop can be timed from the arrival list.** The seven trucks keep their order, so every seventh arrival at the plant is the same truck again. The seven loops from October to December 1917 took 628,000 to 706,000 units (mean 666,000), six from December to February 628,000 to 659,000, and those from March to April 696,000 to 719,000. The window's Frequency followed them ([lines.md](lines.md#the-lines-windows-frequency-rate-and-load-columns-604)).

**The delivery line.**

- **The old bunch came back short.** Due at about 60.8 million on its 3.45 million cycle, it brought only three trucks, which loaded 6 each from 60,790,000 to 61,142,000, and the warehouse stock rose from 92 to 110. That the whole bunch would come was a prediction, and it failed.
- **The new trucks emptied the warehouse at once.** Bought at 61,148,400, they loaded at 61,354,000 and 61,452,000 in two entries of 72 and 38 (six trucks and three trucks plus 2, stamped together), then 3, 3 and 6, and the warehouse read 0. The rest left with nothing. The 110 reached the town stop from 62,698,000 to 62,935,000 in entries of 24, 24, 24, 24 and 14, so a new truck's trip one way is about 1.35 to 1.5 million units, against about 1.7 million for the old trucks.
- **Cargo on a vehicle that is sold leaves no trace in the lists.** The old trucks' last loads (6 + 6 + 6, then 3 + 3 + 6) never reached the town stop, and the line's lists still counted those 30 as in transit after the old trucks were sold, when the window's Load showed only the new trucks' 110. One of the old trucks left parked in the depot showed a load of 0, so the 30 went with the trucks that were sold. No list, lost or destroyed, records them. The stone line shows the same: before its carts were replaced the lists put 16 to 20 stone and 4 to 8 cement in transit, about the window's Load; from then on they count 23 more than the window (17 stone and 6 cement, what the carts held at 58.6 million): 72 against a Load of 49 in March 1919 ([below](#two-convoys-and-a-stone-line-that-spreads-out-again-604)). **Observed**, two lines, both times a whole batch sold: a save made just before and after selling one loaded vehicle would settle it.

**Two jams on the road into the warehouse's station.**

- Both lines use the same station by the warehouse, and the road into it is also the way out of the depot where the new trucks were bought. Each time a batch of new trucks left the depot together, the player saw them queue on that road, and the stone trucks waited behind them.
- **The lists show each jam as a gap in the stone trucks' cement drop-offs at the station**, followed by several trucks unloading together. The drop-offs came every 90,000 to 100,000 units until 61,264,000, then none until 61,600,000, when an entry of 18 (three trucks) arrived; the loop of the truck caught in it took 861,000 units against 628,000 to 659,000 for the six before. After the second batch of 13 trucks (bought at 62,722,000) there was a drop-off on 21 April 1918, a single one on 6 May, and then an entry of 12 (two trucks) on 22 May, when the player saw the road clear. The plant's arrival list shows the first holdup a little later, as a gap of 327,000 units. Two more gaps of about the same size, in the drop-offs from 7 to 27 March 1918 and in the plant's arrivals from 6 to 23 April, came with no new trucks: they are the first jam's bunch coming round again ([below](#two-convoys-and-a-stone-line-that-spreads-out-again-604)).

## Two convoys and a stone line that spreads out again (604)

**Observed** in four later saves of the same game (clock 64.4 to 68.5 million, August 1918 to April 1919, calendar 0.25x): an autosave of 10 August 1918, a paused save of 4 March 1919 and two autosaves of 25 April 1919 written at the same paused clock, with the player's Lines tab on 16 August 1918 and 4 March 1919. The player changed nothing on either line. The windows' figures are in [lines.md](lines.md#the-lines-windows-frequency-rate-and-load-columns-604).

**The delivery line runs as two convoys, one per batch of trucks.**

- **The batches take turns at the warehouse.** The April batch loaded 72 + 6 at 62,930,000 to 62,975,000, and then no truck loaded until the January batch came back at 64,158,000 and took 24, 24, 36 and 12 (to 64,504,000), what the stock held. The April batch loaded 102 from 65,740,000 to 66,022,000, the January batch 96 from 67,032,000 to 67,495,000. Each convoy takes what the stock holds, so most of its trucks leave empty.
- **A round trip takes about 2.8 to 2.9 million units** (62,930,000 to 65,740,000 for the April batch, 64,158,000 to 67,032,000 for the January batch), and one way about 1.4 million (the April batch's 102 reached the town stop from 67,139,000 to 67,490,000). Between visits the warehouse fills with what the stone line drops off: 12 after the January batch left, 72 at 68.5 million.

**The stone line bunched in the second jam and spread out again.**

- **After the May 1918 jam the seven trucks ran as a group.** The line's `itemsAtStop1_c24` list shows seven arrivals 52,000 to 76,000 units apart and then nothing for about 250,000 units. The gap came round once a loop, at 63.29, 63.93 and 64.64 million in the plant's arrival list, then shrank away. From 66.2 million the arrivals were evenly spaced, 89,000 to 91,000 units apart, and each truck's loop took 628,000 to 633,000 units, against 696,000 to 740,000 while the trucks were bunched.
- **The first jam did the same.** Gaps begin in the plant's arrivals at 61.70 and 62.40 million (697,000 apart, about one loop) and in the station's cement drop-offs at 61.90 and 62.62 million (721,000 apart), the March and April gaps [above](#replacing-both-lines-vehicles-604). So a bunch made by a jam keeps its shape for several loops.
- **The chain's flow.** In each 1,461,000-unit period from 61.4 to 67.2 million the quarry loaded 168 to 192 stone and the plant made 90 to 96 cement. The quarry destroyed none after 58.7 million.

### Town buildings in the later saves

- **Eleven buildings got cement for the first time** when the new trucks' first loads arrived: their keyed groups begin at 62.70 to 62.82 million. There were 32 such groups in the August 1918 save and 33 in April 1919, against 23 in December 1916.
- **Building lists lose cargo between saves.** One 1916 group (13 cement) is missing from the later saves. Read from the August 1918 save, the gap between the town stop and the buildings is 276 at clock 54.6 million (246 in the 1916 save), 287 by 62 million and 283 to 287 up to 64.4 million, the small changes being cement between the stop and a building. Where the other 17 went is **Open**.
