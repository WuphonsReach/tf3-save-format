# Vehicles

Where a save keeps a vehicle and what happens to that record in play: its model instance (position and heading), how to tell which instance is which vehicle, and what a depot, a line, a purchase or a sale does to it. Checked on 604 in one game, the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game).

Other notes hold the rest of a vehicle's trail:

- Its name slot and entity id, and how the two relate within one purchase: [entity-names.md](entity-names.md#vehicles-model-name-slot-and-entity-id).
- Its count in the load dialog: [header.md](header.md#counts-the-load-dialog-shows).
- Purchase and sale bookings: [finances.md](finances.md#selling-a-vehicle-is-a-positive-booking-in-the-purchase-category-604); running costs, the parked rate and condition steps: [running-costs.md](running-costs.md).
- Passenger trip records that name a vehicle: [lines.md](lines.md#trip-plans-line-stops-and-vehicle-or-a-building-604).

## Vehicle instances (604)

**Observed** on 604, one game (two saves first, 4 ships and 3 horse carts on the map, then later saves of the same game). Each vehicle on the map has one model instance of the kind in [models.md](models.md#the-model-instance-record), in one run with the animals and people ([models.md](models.md#vehicles-people-and-animals-in-one-run)).

So a vehicle's position and model are in this run, though still not next to its entity id ([entity-names.md](entity-names.md#vehicles-model-name-slot-and-entity-id)).

### Finding a ship and reading its position

- **Find a vehicle** by its model: look up the model's id in the model table (the ships' id was 4192), then scan the stream for that `u32` followed by a 4 x 4 `f32` matrix whose floats 3, 7 and 11 are 0 and float 15 is 1. The four ships gave exactly four hits, 146 bytes apart.
- **Ships keep their order, and the order is the vehicle number.** Before sailing (a save with all four in the depot) the four instances held one identical position and heading, the ship depot's spawn point; later each had its own, so the slot order is stable. Slot 4 is Ship 4: the player described Ship 4 as loaded (10/10) and waiting outside the port area for Ship 2 to finish unloading and leave, Ship 2 as at the port and Ships 1 and 3 as empty and heading out, and the saved positions and headings agree (below). The journal agrees as well: the vehicle running-cost bookings come in the same order ([running-costs.md](running-costs.md#upkeep-and-running-costs-are-booked-in-batches-604)), and the fourth one sums to Ship 4's own figure. The slot with the ship at the port sat at 410.8 and 4,986.7, 120 to 370 m from the carts at the nearby stop. **Observed**, one game with four ships.
- **Coordinates.** Values ran from -1,548 to 1,427 in x and -4,926 to 5,450 in y over the whole run, with ships at z = 0 (water level) and land things at 3 to 8. The map is 4,608 x 13,824 m, so the origin is the map's centre, not a corner, which is where the heightmap puts it ([terrain.md](terrain.md#the-heightmap)).
- The matrix's top-left 2 x 2 holds the heading as a rotation about the vertical axis, and the first row (floats 0 and 1) is the direction the ship faces. In the save the four ships read: slot 1, (1, 0) at x 1,108, y 4,152, the farthest from the port, facing east, away from it; slot 2, (0.27, 0.96) at the port; slot 3, (0.14, -0.99) 380 m south of the port, facing south, away from it; slot 4, (-0.99, 0.14) at x 613, y 4,950, about 200 m from the port, facing west towards it. That is outbound, at the port, outbound and queued for the port, as the player saw. A ship waiting for a free berth is therefore still stored at a position, facing its stop. **Observed**, one save. Slot 1 held the identity rotation exactly, which may only mean it faced due east.
- The second (previous-frame) matrix held the same translation and rotation as the first in all four ships, so the save, made with the game paused, gives no speed or direction of travel apart from the heading. The other bytes of the four instances (the flag byte between the matrices and the 13 bytes after them) were identical, so no velocity is in the record either. A search for 8 km/h as an `f32` or `f64` in m/s (2.22) found nothing in the stream, but the window rounds, so that proves little. **Observed**, one save; where a ship's speed is kept is **Open**.
- **A second save, 62.4 s later** (clock 632,200 against 569,800, paused again): the four ships read, with the player's description of each: slot 1 at x 1,326, y 4,147, "sitting at the fishing grounds" (218 m east of where it was); slot 2 at 390.4, 4,910.9 facing (-0.39, -0.92), "leaving the port" (78 m south-west of the port); slot 3 at 703, 4,158 facing (0.99, -0.12), "heading out to the fishing area" (east, towards slot 1); slot 4 at 470.9, 4,972.5 facing (-0.98, 0.22), "heading into the port, not far out now" (61 m east of the port, facing it). All four headings agree with what the player saw, so the first row of the matrix is the facing direction. The ships' windows in that save read 26 km/h for Ship 4 and 35 km/h for Ship 3. Ship 3 had moved at least 563 m in the 62.4 s since the earlier save (the straight line between its two positions), 9.0 m/s or 32.5 km/h, so a speed of 35 km/h fits clock units being milliseconds. Ship 4's 143 m in the same time is 8.3 km/h on average, which is lower than its 26 km/h because it waited for a berth for part of it (that it equals the 8 km/h its window showed a save earlier is chance). One pair of ships, so a rough check. The previous-frame matrix again equalled the current one. **Observed**, one game.
- The 9 bytes between records and the other bytes of an instance are still **Open**.

### Ships in a depot, bought, parked and added

- **A vehicle in a depot has no instance.** A later save (six ships) held five ship instances: the four `british_columbia` ships and one `sydfart` at sea, 1,266 and 4,581. The sixth ship, a `dunara_castle` standing in a ship depot ("In Depot" in its window), had none. So that vehicle, in a depot, had no instance, but not every vehicle in a depot lacks one: see the next bullet. The player's reading is that a ship in a depot that is **not assigned to a line** has no instance, which fits both observations (the `dunara_castle` had no line; the ships of the next bullet had one). **Observed**, one ship.
- **Ships bought and not yet sent do have instances, all at one point.** In save `1312` (made right after two new ship lines were set up, none of the new ships sailing yet, according to the player) the journal had 13 new ship purchases, and the stream had 13 new ship instances: 8 of `british_columbia` (the model of the 256,032 purchases) and 5 of `sydfart` (the 438,912 ones, the model of the old Ship 6). All 8 of the first model read one position (x 861.1, y 4,761.4, z 0) and one heading (0.99, -0.16), all 5 of the second another (869.4, 4,760.1) with the same heading, 8 m apart, and the four old ships and the old Ship 6 stayed where they were in the save before. So a depot's spawn point is stored as the position of every vehicle waiting there, as in the first test above. These ships had been put on a line (TOD Fishery 2 and 3, see [lines.md](lines.md#a-new-line-and-its-vehicles)); the `dunara_castle` that had no instance had not. The player's reading is that it is the line assignment that gives a waiting vehicle an instance. The tests so far do not settle it. After the player took Ship 8 (of Ships 7 to 11, line TOD Fishery 2) and Ship 18 (of Ships 12 to 19, TOD Fishery 3) off their lines, as the line lists in the screenshots show, the saves `1322` and `1323` (paused at clock 5,997,000) held all 18 ship instances (12 `british_columbia`, 6 `sydfart`) with the ship records byte for byte the same as in `1312`; `1324` (clock 6,003,400) and `1327` (6,084,200) still held 12 and 6, with ships leaving the depot one at a time. In `1327` one `sydfart` was still on the spawn point and was taken to be the unassigned Ship 8, but in `1328` (clock 6,215,400) all five new `sydfart` ships were sailing east, so that one was waiting for its turn and not Ship 8. The player's line windows after the run show why: Ship 8 and Ship 18 were back on TOD Fishery 2 and 3 (Ships 7 to 11 and 12 to 19 again), without the player putting them back. So taking a ship off its line while the game is paused and it is in the depot did not last once the simulation ran, and the ship left with the others. In `1328` three of the eight `british_columbia` (Ships 12 to 19) had left, as the player counted, and five were still on the spawn point, which is the four assigned ships still waiting and the unassigned Ship 18 if it kept its instance; the save cannot tell which is which, since an unassigned ship and a waiting one look the same (same position, same heading). The test of the idea therefore needs a ship that stays unassigned while the game runs (a new ship bought and not given a line). Why the `dunara_castle` had none is **Open**. Where a ship's line is stored was not found: any two paused saves of this game differ by tens of thousands of bytes in the statistics area (even `1322` against `1323`), so a byte diff does not isolate the change.
- **A second test: a ship sent to a depot with no line.** Ship 18 was ticked alone and sent to a depot with "Send Selected Vehicle to Depot" (its line colour went and a depot icon showed in the list), and it appeared in the depot list of the ship depot "East", beside Ship 5, the `dunara_castle` that has no line either. Saves `1333` (clock 6,242,200), `1334` (6,287,000) and `1335` (6,304,600) each held 12 `british_columbia` instances, which is the four old ships and all eight new ones, Ship 18 among them, so a ship in a depot with no line did not lose its instance. In `1335` exactly one of the 12 had not moved since `1334` (17,600 units earlier): at (861.6, 4,761.3), 0.5 m from the first depot's spawn point, heading (0.99, -0.16); all the others, including the `sydfart` ships, had moved. That stationary instance is the best candidate for Ship 18 (the same slot was one of the four on the spawn point in `1333`, the others of which left), but the save does not name it, so it rests on elimination. The `dunara_castle` still had no instance in `1335`. The player recalls that Ship 5 was a large ship bought for the first fishing line, found too big for the `STATIONS_WATER_SMALL_PIER` (Small Landing) and sent to the depot afterwards, so it got there the way Ship 18 did. The next save, `1339` (clock 6,428,600), held 11 `british_columbia` instances, not 12, and the stationary one at (861.6, 4,761.3) was gone: Ship 18's instance was dropped some time after the ship was parked. The running-cost bookings put Ship 18 in the depot at about 6,280,000 ([running-costs.md](running-costs.md#a-ship-parked-in-a-depot-pays-5-of-its-running-costs-604)), so it still had its instance 24,600 units later (`1335`, 6,304,600) and no longer 148,600 units later; where in between it went is not known (no save between). So the player's idea holds, with a delay: a ship that is parked in a depot with no line ends up with no instance, as Ship 5 has none. The earlier unassigned test was not valid (Ships 8 and 18 were back on their lines when the game ran, see above), and this one is the clean case. A ship waiting at a depot on a line keeps its instance at the spawn point. **Observed**, one ship against another, identification of Ship 18 by elimination.
- **Later autosaves added no instance back** (**Observed**, same game). Three autosaves at play speed 4, 216,800, 1,416,800 and 2,617,600 units after `1339` (clock 6,645,400, 7,845,400 and 9,046,200), each held 17 ship instances: 11 `british_columbia`, 6 `sydfart`, no `dunara_castle`. So Ship 18 and Ship 5 stayed without an instance for the 2.6 million units, and nothing else dropped or appeared. The autosaves are 1.2 million units apart at 4x, so they cannot narrow when Ship 18's instance went (between 6,304,600 and 6,428,600). Several instances sat on the same two points in different slots in different saves ((411, 4,987) and (19, 4,317)); ships probably waiting at a stop or port (not checked), so a coordinate shared by two slots does not mark the depot spawn point by itself.
- **The slot order is not the vehicle number once vehicles have been added.** The first four `british_columbia` ships sit in the 146-byte block as before, but the 8 new ones and the new `sydfart` ships are scattered through the instance data, 3,000 to 97,000 bytes apart, with the old Ship 6 in the middle of the `sydfart` ones (after three new ships and before two). So the instances are in slot order, and new ones take free slots as entity names do ([entity-names.md](entity-names.md#what-a-rename-and-new-building-do-to-the-table-604)); which new ship is which number was not worked out. **Observed**, one save.

### Road vehicles: the same order between saves, and a parked bus

**Observed**, one game (the Small subarctic game, saves `1437` to `1441`, 20 `landauer_v2` buses, 3 of them tracked by the player on one bus line). Matched by order, the instances moved by distances that fit the speed in the vehicle window: at 20 km/h, 4.4 s of clock (11,324,600 to 11,329,000) gave 24.0 to 24.5 m for the buses at full speed, and shorter distances for those at stops. Two saves with the same clock (`1438` and `1439`) held identical instances. One bus (Road Vehicle 27) was ordered to a depot; the save with it standing in the depot had it at the depot's own position, 12 m from the depot building's model instance (`depots/road/road_depot/road_depot.mdl`, the depot's translation read the same way, with a second `road_depot_sm` instance at the same point). Before the order it had stood 28 m from a road maintenance station (`depots/road/road_maint_station/road_maint_station.mdl`), which is where the player saw it. So a depot and a maintenance station are placed things with their own instances and can be used to tell which slot is which vehicle. The bus kept its instance at the depot in that save (clock 11,983,800), but not in the next one, 299,200 units later with no change made in between (clock 12,283,000): 19 `landauer_v2` instances instead of 20, none within 60 m of the depot point, and the other 18 in the same order as before. So a road vehicle drops its instance after parking, as the ship of the test above did (between 24,600 and 148,600 units after parking); the bus was still driving at clock 11,791,000 and parked by 11,983,800, so it kept the instance for less than 492,000 units after arriving, a bound not narrowed further. **Assigned to the line again** (save `1443`, same clock 12,283,000 as the save before it, paused, one change): the instance was back, 20 `landauer_v2` again, at 126.6 and 4,949.4, 24 m from the depot's own point and 12 m from where the bus had parked, facing the opposite way (0.64, -0.77 against the parked -0.64, 0.77, which is the depot building's own heading). It stands at another place in the run, slot 10 instead of 19, so the order of the run is not kept when an instance is dropped and made again, and the other 19 instances were identical to the earlier save. **Tracking it afterwards by order** (saves `1443`, `1444`, `1445`, the count staying at 20): the 20 instances kept one order, every step fitted the speed (the longest, 3,511 m in 932,400 units, against 5.2 km at 20 km/h), and slot 10 read as the bus at each save: at the depot (127, 4,949), then 391 m away at the first stops of the line in the depot's town (-190, 5,180; the player said it sat in traffic behind trucks there), then at (-1,150, 1,910), 3,408 m away (3.66 m/s, 13 km/h, which is the speed the window showed) and 6 m from a `small_old` stop in the line's other town, where the player saw it just leaving the stop with six passengers. The stops are `stations/street/small_stops/small_old.mdl` instances (17 in that game), and no instance of a stop sign model existed. In that game the depot's town (the player's "Todmorden") lies at the north-east end of the connecting road (stops at x -170 to 120, y 4,980 to 5,260) and the other town ("Retford") at the south-west end (stops at x -1,265 to -1,150, y 1,450 to 1,920), so a town's position can be read from the stop instances of its lines. Its six passenger records read `(0, 1)`, from the first stop to the depot's town ([lines.md](lines.md#trip-plans-line-stops-and-vehicle-or-a-building-604)). The order itself (the "go to depot" mark) was not found: all 192 vehicle instances were identical before and after it.

## Road vehicles bought: an instance at the depot at once (604)

**Observed**, one game (the Small subarctic game; seven saves). The road vehicle models sit under both `vehicle/road/` and `vehicle/truck/` in the table (`horse_cart_small` is `vehicle/truck/horse_cart_small/horse_cart_small.mdl`), so scan every `vehicle/` model, not one folder.

- All seven vehicles of one line were sold: the next save had seven instances fewer (three `horsewagon_1850_v2` gone, four `horsewagon_1850_usa_v2` gone) and nothing else changed.
- Five Small Horse Carriages (`horse_cart_small`) were then bought and the game saved paused at once. The save held five new instances (4 became 9 of that model), **all at one point**, 25 m from the road depot's own instance, with the game paused. So a bought road vehicle has its model instance at the moment of purchase, like the ships bought and not yet sent above.
- After about a second of game time at 1x, one of the five had moved 2 m and four were still at the spawn point. In the save with all five on the road, they sat 25 to 150 m from the spawn point along the road, and two saves later (the first one at its first stop, loading) they were 5 to 60 m further on, still short of the line's first stop. The name slots and the header count of the new vehicles are there at purchase too ([entity-names.md](entity-names.md), [header.md](header.md#counts-the-load-dialog-shows)).
- Where the cargo of a vehicle is stored (the first `horse_cart_small` had 1 of 4 loaded in the last save) was not looked for.
- **Seven trucks bought in one go (saves `1958` and `1959`, same clock).** The model `benz1912_box` (a 1912 truck, id 4345 in that save's table) had no instance before and seven after, within 0.6 m of one point at the Todmorden road depot (x 125.2 to 125.8, y 4,950.4 to 4,951.1), so a bought road vehicle is placed at its depot at once like the ships and carts above. The seven sit 5,904 to 238,371 bytes apart in the stream (free slots taken as in the ship tests). In the same save the seven horse carts that were sold left no visible trace: counts of every `vehicle/` model were equal except the seven new trucks (`horse_cart_small` 11 and 11, `horsewagon_1850_usa_v2` 5 and 5). The player says the carts were sold while out on their line route, not in a depot, so they had instances, and a sold vehicle's instance stayed in the stream after the sale at the same clock tick. **An autosave 440,800 clock units later** (clock 43,802,000, 18 August 1912, play speed 4) had `horse_cart_small` at 4 instead of 11, the seven trucks still at 7, the road vehicle count still 83, and `horsewagon_1850_usa_v2` (5) and every other vehicle model unchanged apart from cars and people, which come and go. So the sold carts were `horse_cart_small`, and their instances were dropped some time in those 440,800 units (the header count had dropped at the sale). When within that span was not narrowed, and what happens to a vehicle between the sale and the drop was not watched: the player thinks a vehicle sold on the road does not drive back to a depot, but has not tracked one after such a sale (so the parked bus above, which lost its instance 299,200 units after reaching a depot, may not be a comparable case). **Observed**, one game; the lag and the vehicle's fate are **Open**.
