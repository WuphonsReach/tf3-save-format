# Map editor saves: tests

The dated tests behind [editor-saves.md](editor-saves.md): what a map editor save looks like when loaded and saved at once, a blank template with a road test, and later saves one change apart (deletes, roads, a tree, a farm, a rock). All on 604, in two editor maps.

## What the tests showed

The conclusions, with their labels and what is still **Open**, are in [In short](editor-saves.md#in-short). The sections below are the saves they come from, in the order the player made them.

## What editing in the map editor writes

Observed on 604, one Giant temperate editor map, twelve saves one change apart. Road edits (a deleted segment, a split road, a new road, a new street type) are in the street-edge table and the tables around it: [road-network.md](road-network.md). Ticking Historic Preservation on three buildings changed three flag bytes and nothing else in their records ([town-buildings.md](editor-map-buildings.md)). The animal list was the same in the saves compared, and changed by one bird's height after a town was deleted, so its positions can serve as landmarks. Deleting a town left its roads, buildings and town record in the stream ([road-edits.md](road-edits.md#deleting-the-town)).

### Loading an editor save and saving it at once

**Observed** on 604, once: a save loaded in the game and saved with no edit (`0648` from `0647` of the [Giant temperate editor game](test-games.md#giant-temperate-editor-game)). The header was the same in every field read (money 0, counter 1678, the 12 mods, the settings), the street-edge table and the animal list were identical, and the stream was 2,870 bytes longer. The differences were:

- A 4-byte value inside records of 12 bytes, over about 1.8 MB near the end of the stream (some 120,000 values), went from 8 to 10. What it counts is **Open**.
- In about 130 places in two tables in the middle of the stream a record of 40 or 27 bytes moved past its neighbours (the stream grew by its size at one point and shrank by it a record later), so these look like sets whose order changed.
- 17 ids joined the free-id list ([road-network.md](road-network.md#the-other-tables-a-road-edit-touches)).
- A block of Lua-encoded table data, 2.8 KB, was added at the end of the stream, with the keys `ProgressPage`, `saved` and `trackLists`; which state it belongs to was not checked.

So a load and re-save is not a byte copy, and a diff between an edited save and the one before it holds these changes too; compare with a no-edit re-save first.

Editor maps played as a game (Save and Play, a flag flip): [editor-play.md](editor-play.md).

## A blank template and a road test

**Observed** on 604, one Tiny tropical map ([Tiny flat editor game](test-games.md#tiny-flat-editor-game)): the template as the editor saves it, and two saves made from it by hand.

- **The blank template holds almost nothing.** Counter 0, a 5.0 MB stream, no town records, no industries, no street edges and no parcel table (`street_edges.py`, `parcels.py` and `tf3save.scan_records` find none). The ground is the heightmap alone. Find a blank template by that: a map-size header with no town, edge or parcel table.
- **The first save after drawing is a different file in kind.** With two towns, three industries (a farm, a forest, a saw mill), a country road between the towns and short spurs to the industries, the stream was 13.9 MB, the counter 488, the street-edge table held 147 edges and the parcel table 1,474 parcels on 67 street edges. The header was otherwise unchanged, `isMapEditor` still true.
- **Drawing bypasses re-cuts the road they leave.** The next save added four new curves joining the country road at both ends, of the types `town_old_medium`, `town_old_large`, `town_old_small` and the `country_old_*_barriers` types, 53 edges in all. Eight of the 147 edges went and none of the others changed: the 80 m edges of the road at each junction were replaced by edges ending at the new junction nodes, with the same total length (80 + 80 became 95 + 65; 160 m between two old nodes became 71 + 89). The old road stayed in its place, so a bypass adds a path and takes none away. The parcel table was identical in both saves: the new roads ran through open ground and added no parcel.
- **A node id was reused (once).** The node at 591 m south of the origin in the first save was gone from the second, and the same id stood 790 m further south as a four-edge junction. Whether the game reuses the ids of removed nodes in general is **Open** ([road-network.md](road-network.md#the-other-tables-a-road-edit-touches) holds the free-id list).

The edge layout and street types are in [road-network.md](road-network.md) and the glossary has the labels ([glossary.md](glossary.md)).

### A delete reaches the edge table with the next new road

**Observed** on 604, one Tiny tropical editor map, eight saves one step apart after the road test above: a save with no edit, a deleted road piece, a three-edge wide road drawn, its middle edge deleted, a short isolated road drawn, then a save with no edit, a deleted piece (the isolated road) and a save with no edit. (Another map: [road-edits.md](road-edits.md#what-the-edits-did).)

- **A no-edit re-save differs from its parent in the preview and about 13 small places.** The stream length was the same; the first 691 KB (the preview picture, 640 x 360 x 3 bytes) changed and under 200 other bytes in about a dozen short runs. The street-edge and parcel tables were identical.
- **A deleted road piece was still in the street-edge table in the save made seconds after the delete.** Three deletes (a piece of a 90 m town road, one of three 86 m wide-road edges, and the 34 m isolated road) had gone from the screen when the save was made, yet each edge table equalled its parent's. The first two edges went from the table in the save that also held the next new road. After the third delete, two saves (the second with no edit) still held the edge, so a save alone does not clear it; whether the next road drawn anywhere does is the likeliest reading, as both clears came with a road drawn 50 m or more from the deleted piece. A tree and passing days did not clear it, a farm with its own roads did ([below](#a-running-clock-a-tree-a-farm-and-a-rock)).
- **A list beside the edge-addon names gets one entry per delete at once.** The table of `infrastructure/edge_addons/*.edge` names (`barrier_a.edge` and others, each with an index) ends with a count (2 in the first saves) and a list of 21-byte entries: an 8-byte id, then the 13 bytes `02 06 02 00 03` and eight zeros. Each of five deletes (the first in `1940`, the last in `1951`) raised the count by 1 and appended an entry, in no other save; the ids were `0x156a`, `0x38ba`, `0x77c`, `0x3809`, `0xdaa`, not in order. So the delete is recorded straight away while the edge table lags. What the ids name is **Open** (none was found as a `u32` anywhere else in the stream). Find the list from the name `barrier_track_b.edge` and the 8 bytes `05 00 00 00 12 00 00 00` after it.
- **The new edge took the deleted edge's slot.** In both cases that had a new road the first new edge was written at the byte offset where the deleted record had stood (to a few bytes, the records differing in size); further new edges were appended at the end of the table.
- **The dead end stays.** The nodes at both ends of a deleted piece stayed in the table as one-edge nodes; nothing joined their neighbours.
- **A drawn road takes node ids from gaps.** The wide road's four nodes were three ids below the highest in use (in runs of unused ids) and one above it, so ids are not handed out in order. The isolated road's two nodes were both above the highest.
- **The junction table showed two of the three deletes at once** ([junctions.md](junctions.md) has its layout). Its record count followed the nodes drawn (178, 182, 184 patterns across the saves) and did not drop at a delete, because the dead ends stayed. After the first delete the record of the node between the deleted piece and its neighbour lost its edge id and its two turns (66 bytes to 26); after the second, the last two records (nodes of the wide road, from 6 turns to 0, 130 bytes to 22) lost all their turns and six other records held the same ids in another order (same length; which nodes they are was not read). After the third delete, of an isolated road, no record changed.
- **The strip table did not change in any of the eight saves** (64 entries, 1,297 rows): none of the roads drawn or deleted, country roads among them, touched it.
- **The parcel table did not change in any of the eight saves.** A deleted piece of a town road and roads in open ground touched no parcel.
- **Each edit made the stream 56 to 74 KB longer, in three places** (found by matching 48-byte anchors between neighbouring saves; the three saves after the first delete show the same three). First the ground-texture polygon list (`*.gtex` names, [road-network.md](road-network.md#the-other-tables-a-road-edit-touches)), 13.6 to 15.7 KB: a delete added the same 13,559 bytes of records twice, for two different edges (the first at a 90 m piece, the second at an 86 m one), and a drawn road 14.6 to 15.7 KB. Then two unnamed lists of binary floats, one about 7 KB at roughly 40 per cent of the stream and one 37 to 50 KB at 85 to 95 per cent, each growing in every edit. What they hold is **Open**. A save with no edit grew none of them, and the third delete (the isolated road) changed none of the three: its saves held the same polygon records and the same stream length to within 31 bytes.

### Standalone and attached roads, tram types, a road re-cut

**Observed** on 604, the same map, six more saves one step apart (draw an 86 m standalone road, delete it, draw a 153 m road across two roads, draw a 71 m road off it, delete the 71 m road, a save with no edit); the thumbnails of the saves show each step.

- **The lag again.** The 86 m road's edge was still in the table in the save made after its delete and went in the next, which held the next road drawn; the 71 m road's edge stayed in the save of its delete and in a no-edit save after it. The earlier standalone road (34 m) left in the save of the next road drawn, so all five deletes in the two series follow the rule above.
- **A standalone delete is 31 bytes.** Deleting a road not joined to any other made the stream 31 bytes shorter, twice (the 34 m and the 86 m road), though the new list entry is 21 bytes longer, so 52 bytes went elsewhere (not located); no table of edges, polygons or junctions changed. Deleting the attached 71 m road cut 7.1 KB and changed no polygon record: the only delete that shortened the stream by more than 31 bytes.
- **A flushed standalone road takes its nodes with it, and their ids are reused.** The 34 m road's two nodes (`fe6`, `fe7`) were gone from the edge table once it flushed and were the ids of two nodes of the 153 m road drawn two saves later. A deleted piece of a joined road leaves its end nodes as dead ends (above).
- **Tram types are street types.** A standalone road of the 86 m kind drawn as a tram road (`town_old_medium_tram`, one edge of 85.5 m) and a tram-only road of 71.5 m (`tram_old`) are in the street-edge table with the other road types.
- **A road is re-cut where another joins it.** The 153 m road (two edges of 76.7 m, type `town_old_medium_trees`) became three edges of 51 m (52.3, 50.5, 50.5) when the 71 m road met it at a new node; the main road's two edges at the other end (97 m and 80 m) became three (72.7, 52.1 and 52.1 m). Each total length stayed the same.
- **Stream sizes in these six saves:** +14.8 KB (standalone road), -31 B (its delete), -27.0 KB (the 153 m road drawn, the 86 m road flushed), +54.5 KB (the 71 m road, the largest step so far) and -7.1 KB (its delete), then 0.
- **Parcels:** none changed in any of the six.

### A running clock, a tree, a farm and a rock

**Observed** on 604, the same map, eight more saves (an autosave, then `1953` to `1959`: a delete of a standalone road, an exotic tree, a small livestock farm, a curved road, a rock raised 50 units above the ground, the rock deleted, a fruit tree). The editor map was now running: the autosave after `1952` held play speed 1 and the date 1900-03-15, and each later save had a later day (to 1900-05-17), so the stream differs by the days passed as well as by the edit, and the byte counts below are not clean.

- **The edge table lag held through a tree and a running clock.** The deletes of `1951` (71 m road) and `1953` (a standalone road) were both still in the edge table in the saves of `1952`, the autosave, `1953` and `1954` (an exotic large tree). Both went in `1955`, the save with the farm. A farm brings its own roads: three edges, two of `ind_country_old_small` (57.9 and 92.1 m) and one `ind_entrance_old_country` (12.5 m), that reach the farm's front. So a placement flushes the deleted edges when it brings road edges of its own, and a tree, a rock and passing days do not.
- **A road drawn off a farm re-cuts the road it meets.** The curved road in `1956` (two edges of 89.4 m, `country_old_medium`) ran from the farm's road to a diagonal town road, which was cut at the junction node into 41.1 and 49.1 m. The farm's entrance edge was written again with the same line and a new end node id, `fa3` in place of `1056`; `fa3` was an id freed in `1955` with the flush of the deleted wide-road piece.
- **A road drawn beside a farm can remove one of its fields** (**Observed**, once; your screenshots of `1955` and `1956` show the lower right field gone where the new road ran along its edge). In the stream the farm's fields are Lua-encoded module tables, one per field, each beginning with the string `::/industries/livestock_farm/field.module` (the other farm types use their own folder, `farm/`, `forest/`) and a `variant` key (0 in the records read in [farm-fields.md](farm-fields.md); the number that tells fields apart is the slot, the table key before each record). `1955` held seven records of that string (the numbers 3, 4, 7, 8, 12 and 15 were read after the `variant` key, so they were probably the next record's slot, not variants; the seventh record carried a `seed` and a `sizeFactor` instead); `1956` held six, the one numbered 3 gone and the other five still there. Which field on the screen that is was not read; find the records by that string and count them. Its fence, plants and animals left the instance table a save later ([models.md](models.md#trees-rocks-and-farm-fields-are-model-instances)).
- **Trees, a rock and a road delete leave the polygon, parcel and junction tables alone.** The tree saves and the rock saves held the same polygon-record count (2,764 at `1954`, 2,828 after the curved road), no parcel changed anywhere, and the farm added two junction records and 41 polygon records, the curved road one junction record and 23 polygon records.
- **The delete list takes some placements.** The list beside the edge-addon names (the earlier section) rose by one entry for the exotic tree (`1954`), the farm (`1955`) and the fruit tree (`1959`), with ids that are negative (-100, -441, -100), and did not rise for the road delete of `1953`, the curved road, the rock or its delete. Entries also gained a four-byte value after the id in some saves: one for the `1951` delete held 390,000 in `1954`, one for the tree held 437,800 in `1955`, and both were back to zero later. The saves' clock (ticks) is in this range, so the value may be a time; what the list is for is **Open**, and "one entry per delete" above holds for five deletes only.
- **Sizes.** The tree added 3.6 KB, the farm 765 KB, the curved road 75 KB, the rock's place and delete 107 KB shorter then 7.5 KB longer, the fruit tree 0.8 KB; the first running save (the autosave) was 475 KB longer than `1952` with no edit at all. The rock's shorter stream was not traced.

## The model preview block and the Towns / Industries dialog

**Observed** on 604, a Giant subarctic editor map (seed in the header `id`), saves in the order they were made: a base save, one with 11 towns, one after the Towns / Industries tool had generated 12 towns and 32 industries (an export made), then three more later the same evening, a game started from the last of those, and a pause save of it.

- **The block first appears after the 32 generated industries.** The saves up to and including the one with the generated industries held none. The next editor save held 1,115 records in the main run and 24 in a second, 1,331 in all by the scan; the two editor saves after it and the game started from the last of them held the same records, though the model table grew from 4,504 to 4,539 entries in the game. What was done in the editor between the two saves is not recorded. The tool had already been used for the generated industries, so opening it for the first time does not explain the block unless a tab opened only later writes it.
- **The signature finds only the block.** Scanning every instance of the saves for pages of six or more bare, rotation-free records 68 bytes apart with ids rising by one found 1,278 in the main pages; adding short pages beside them gave 1,331, every one at x 0 to 50 and y -7 to 7. Other scale-only instances number 21,000 to 28,000 per save and none lie in such a run.
- **The dialog's preview colours.** In three screenshots of the dialog on the density slider at Medium, Dense and Packed, white icons number 12 (the towns), brown icons 33 (the industries of the save, 33 in the save made just before) and gold icons 4, 6 and 10, so that brown plus gold equals the dialog's industry totals of 37, 39 and 43. The gold ones are the proposals: the saves hold no record of them.
- **Origin, animals.** About 2,800 (earlier save) to 6,600 animal instances (later saves) sit in the same cells; they are not this block (not rotation-free runs, and present before it). Whether they are the `bird_crane` run that [editor-towns.md](editor-towns.md) lists as a tool defect was not compared.
