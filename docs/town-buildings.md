# Town buildings

The record of every building in a town (houses, shops, workplaces): its level, parcels, height, build time, owning town and the "Historic Preservation" flag, and how the records change in play. The town's own record (capacities and cargo) is in [town-records.md](town-records.md). Checked on 604 in one game, the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game), against the player's screenshots of the building windows.

## The building record and "Historic Preservation" (604)

**Observed** on 604 in the Small subarctic game, saves `1427` (clock 11,239,800), `1428` and `1429` (both 11,324,600), with the player's screenshots of a commercial building in the catchment of the port.

### The record and how to list it

- **The window.** A commercial building's window showed a first row of its level (1), the supply of each cargo it takes (fish 100% or 83%, tinned food 0%) and the year (1900), then "Input Stocks" (fish 2 of 2, tinned food 0 of 1), "Customers" (6 of 6, then 5 of 6 at the later look) and a "Historic Preservation" checkbox, whose tooltip reads that it keeps the building's appearance but it can still level up. The player ticked it between `1427` and `1428`.
- **What the game's API says it is.** The `TownBuilding` component (`api/engine.d.tl`) holds `personCapacity`, `stockList` and `construction` (entities), `level` (1 to 4), `parcels` (entities), `depth` (1 to 4), `height`, `town` (entity), `timeBuilt` (clock) and `blockedDevelopment` (boolean, "block town developer from changing this building"). The command that the checkbox runs, `makeTownBuildingSetBlockedDevelopmentCmd` (`api/cmd.d.tl`), sets that boolean, and the game's own comment calls such a building "Historic". So the flag is `blockedDevelopment`, and the building's customers and stocks sit in the entities it points to.
- **Confirmed on 604 (one game, two buildings): the flag is one byte in the building's record, and the records can be listed.** Saves `1428` (the 5 of 6 building ticked, clock 11,324,600) and `1429` (the 8 of 8 building also ticked, same clock 11,324,600, paused throughout) differed in 124 places, and one of them was a single byte going 0 to 1 inside the record below. `1427` had no record with the byte set, `1428` one and `1429` two, so the byte is the "Historic Preservation" tick (`blockedDevelopment`). The player's count agrees: only two buildings on the map had been ticked by `1429`, and the scan finds exactly two set bytes among 569 records.

```
u32  a            an entity id
u32  b            the same id again, or 0xffffffff
u32  level        1 to 4 by the API; 1 and 2 only in this game
u32  d            an entity id (often a - 1)
u8   blockedDevelopment   0 or 1
vec<u32> parcels  u32 n (1 to 4 here), then n entity ids
u32  depth        1 to 4
f32  height       metres
u32  town         the town's entity id
u64  timeBuilt    a clock value, see calendar.md
```

- **The records follow each other with nothing between them.** A record is 41 + 4n bytes, and the distances between consecutive flag bytes were 45, 49, 53 and 57 (n = 1 to 4) for nearly all of 569 records. The order differs from the API's list (the flag sits straight after `level` and one entity, before the parcels). The scan took a `level` byte, an entity, a flag byte of 0 or 1 and a count of 1 to 16, then checked depth, height, a town id and a build time; a find-by-pattern, not by offset.
- **What the 569 were.** Three towns (entity ids 17,912, 17,913 and 17,914, with 181, 82 and 306 records), every building of the game, not only commercial ones. `1427` held 566 records and `1428` 569: the three buildings built in the 91st month. The two ticked buildings are both in town 17,914: the 5 of 6 one has height 23.3 and `timeBuilt` 9,936,000, the 8 of 8 one (the larger) height 25.7 and `timeBuilt` 7,440,000. Build times run from 0 to 11,304,000, and several buildings share one value (5,017,600 for example), so they come in batches. What `a`, `b` and `d` point to was not followed.

### Ticking buildings, save by save

- **Save `1430` (clock 11,324,600 again, paused) after the player ticked a group of buildings.** The scan paired its 569 records with those of `1429` in order: every field other than the flag was equal, 14 flags went from 0 to 1 and none went back, so 16 were set (the player's count was "a whole bunch more"). Of the 14, 12 were in town 17,914 and 2 in town 17,912; 7 had level 1 and 7 level 2, 1 to 4 parcels, depth 1 to 4, heights 5.5 to 22.1 m, and `timeBuilt` of 0 (three buildings that stood at the start), 1,968,000 to 5,712,000 (eleven), 8,256,000 and 10,464,000. The streams differed in length by about 17,000 bytes, so records were paired by order, not offset. The player confirms 14 and says they were random buildings across the town area, a mix of industrial, commercial and residential, so the ticks are not tied to a street. The whole table of 569 records spans about 32,000 bytes and the 16 set flags span 17,700 of them (341 records between the first and last), which is what a scattered pick over a table that size looks like; the record order was not shown to follow position. **Observed**, one game.
- **Save `1431` (clock still 11,324,600): five houses ticked.** Again every other field was equal and exactly 5 flags went 0 to 1, so 21 were set. All five had `b` = 0xffffffff, level 1, 1 to 4 parcels and heights 6.0 to 26.8 m. Of the 21 set records, 12 had `b` equal to `a` and 9 had `b` = 0xffffffff, and the player's 14 of `1430` were a mix of industrial, commercial and residential. Over all 569 records, 253 had `b` equal to `a` and 316 had `b` = 0xffffffff, in all three towns. So `b` probably is the `stockList` of the API (a building that takes cargo has one; a house has none, written as 0xffffffff) and `a`, equal to it when there is one, would be the capacity entity that then doubles as the stock holder; this fits both ticked commercial buildings and all five houses, but only the 5 houses and the 2 commercial buildings were known by type. **Observed**, one game.
- **The five houses were not close together in the table.** The player ticked them "along the same street". Their record numbers were 34, 390, 462, 504 and 517 of 569 and three of them were in town 17,912 and two in 17,914, so neither the table order nor the `town` field puts them together. Either the street runs along a town border, or the `town` field is not the town the player means; which was not settled.
- **Save `1432` (clock still 11,324,600): two more houses.** Exactly 2 flags went 0 to 1, both records with `b` = 0xffffffff (houses), level 1, 1 and 4 parcels, heights 7.2 and 28.0 m, and again one in each of towns 17,912 and 17,914; 23 were set. The player's screenshots showed the window of such a house: its level (1) and year (1900), a "Noise" rating (Excellent), "Residents" (3 of 3) and the ticked "Historic Preservation" box. So a house's window has no input stocks or customers, which fits it having no stock entity in its record. The player had marked these along one street, yet again one is in town 17,912 and one in 17,914, as with the five of `1431`; whether the `town` field is the town the street shown belongs to is **Open**.
- **Save `1458` against `1457` (clock 13,992,600, a second game state, 604): three buildings ticked at once.** The player ticked the home, the shop and the workplace of one walking person (a residential building with 12 residents, a commercial one with 8 customers and an industrial one with 6 workers, windows read at 1457). The listing above found 660 records in each save; exactly three flags went 0 to 1 (ids 32,639, 21,584 and 39,116), and no other record's flag changed, so 28 flags were set before and 31 after. The method of this section held on a second map state. Which of the three is the home, the shop and the work was not read from the save. Three small entries near the end of the stream also changed in the same pair, for building 21,584 (an existing entry whose fourth field went 0 to 1) and for two buildings (21,078 and 20,900) whose flag did not change and which got new entries with that field at 1; what those entries record is **Open**. The no-line records of the trip array ([lines.md](lines.md#trip-plans-line-stops-and-vehicle-or-a-building-604)) name building ids that are not among these 660 records (44 ids, none shared), so the two sets of ids are not the same kind of building.
- **Save `1434` (clock still 11,324,600): a restaurant.** Exactly one flag went 0 to 1, in a record with `b` equal to `a` (a building with a stock), **level 2**, 2 parcels, depth 3, height 9.0 m, `timeBuilt` 0 (it stood at the start of the game), town 17,914; 22 flags were set. The player's screenshot of the commercial building (a restaurant) read level 2 in the first row, fish at 83%, tinned food at 0%, 1900, input stocks of fish 3 of 4 and tinned food 0 of 2, customers 16 of 16 and the ticked box. So the record's level is the number the window shows. Against the level 1 commercial buildings seen earlier (fish 2 of 2 or 1 of 1, tinned food 0 of 1, customers 6 of 6, 8 of 8 and 1 of 1), a level 2 building holds more stock and serves more customers. **Observed**, one building.
- **A stock-less record may not stay a house (player's caution).** The rule "second field 0xffffffff means a house" was read in a 1900 game where every record was level 1 or 2 and the cargo needs were fish and tinned food. The player suspects that residences get cargo demands later in a game; if they do, a house would have a stock entity and look like a commercial record here. So the two shapes tell stock-less from stocked buildings in this save, not residential from commercial in general. Not tested on a later-game save (the catalog saves with towns of level 3 and 4 buildings could check it).
- **Save `1435` (clock still 11,324,600): a store across the street from the restaurant.** Exactly one flag went 0 to 1 (23 set), in a stocked record (`b` equal to `a`) of level 2, 3 parcels, depth 4, height 27.0 m, `timeBuilt` 3,504,000, town 17,914. The player's screenshot showed a four-storey commercial building (a store) with level 2, fish 100%, tinned food 0%, input stocks fish 4 of 4 and tinned food 0 of 2, customers 16 of 16 and the box ticked, so a level 2 commercial building again holds 4 fish and 2 tinned food, 16 customers. The restaurant of `1434` and this store were in records 234 and 281 of 569, 47 apart, both in town 17,914; their `a` values were 24,455 and 24,930. Two buildings facing each other are thus not neighbours in the table, but closer than the street's five houses were to each other, and in the same town; one pair says little. **Observed**.
- **Save `1436` (clock still 11,324,600): a block of four small buildings, and three flags changed.** The player ticked two buildings of a block of four (a commercial building and a house) and expected two; the scan found 3 flags going 0 to 1 (26 set). The records: number 318 (no stock, level 1, 2 parcels, depth 2, 16.1 m, town 17,914, built 8,664,000), number 382 (stocked, level 1, 3 parcels, depth 4, 12.4 m, town 17,912, built 2,257,600) and number 448 (stocked, level 1, 4 parcels, depth 4, 21.6 m, town 17,914, built 4,656,000). The screenshots showed a commercial building of level 1 (input stocks fish 0 of 1 and tinned food 0 of 1, customers 1 of 1, 0% supplied for both cargos, 1900) unticked and then ticked, and a residential building of level 1 (Noise "Mediocre", residents 1 of 1) with the box being ticked. The player then recalled that the third was a commercial building further away, so the three are the house (318), the commercial building of the block and the farther commercial one, which are 382 and 448 in some order; the table does not say which, since the records hold no position. **Observed**.

### What the flag does in the game

- **What the flag does in the game (screenshot only).** With the bulldozer over a stretch of road beside highlighted buildings, the tooltip read "Costs: $225,000", "Reputation: -4%" and "2 Historic Buildings Will Be Removed". The reputation figure matches the `DemolishedTownBuilding` penalty reason of the town states ([town-states.md](town-states.md)); the road cost includes the buildings. Save `1433` (clock still 11,324,600) is the same stretch after the player unticked those two houses: exactly those two records (the ones ticked in `1432`) went from 1 to 0 and nothing else changed, 21 flags remained, and the same preview read "Costs: $225,000" and "Reputation: -4%" without the historic line. So the flag is reversible with the same byte, the preview cost and reputation were the same with the two buildings historic and not, and the extra line is what the flag adds. Whether the actual demolition then costs or loses more with the flag on was not tried. **Observed**, one stretch.
- **The bulldozer takes a whole road segment (player's observation, not verified).** The player can remove only a whole segment of the street, not part of it, which would explain why the preview lists the buildings along the full stretch (the highlight in the screenshot runs from one junction to the next). That would make the street segment one entity, with the "Historic Buildings Will Be Removed" count taken over every building beside it.

### The town field and the town boundaries

- **Save `1437` (clock still 11,324,600) settles the town field: it is the owning town, and the ids follow the names table (Confirmed on 604, one game, two buildings).** The player ticked an industrial building in Retford and a house in Bromsgrove. Two flags went 0 to 1 (28 set): record 48 (stocked, level 1, 3 parcels, depth 4, 12.9 m, built 0) with town **17,912**, and record 166 (no stock, level 1, 2 parcels, depth 2, 9.5 m, built 0) with town **17,913**. With Todmorden's 17,914 from the earlier buildings, the three ids are Retford 17,912, Bromsgrove 17,913 and Todmorden 17,914, which is the order of the towns in the names table (slots 1 to 3, company id plus slot, see [entity-names.md](entity-names.md)). Then the 6 earlier ticks that carried 17,912 (three of the five houses of `1431`, two buildings of `1430` and one commercial building of `1436`) are Retford buildings by their record, and the 20 with 17,914 are Todmorden's. The player had taken all of them for Todmorden, and says a single road joins the two towns and that each town has a clear boundary on the map (it decides whose area pollution falls in, and removing something on the boundary affects both towns). So the six are not explained by streets crossing at several places; one possibility is that the street of the five houses is that connecting road, with the boundary on it (three of the five were Retford's, two Todmorden's), which the player has not checked. **Open**. The screenshots: an industrial building window with level 1, one input stock (0 of 2 of a cargo shown as a sack), workers 8 of 8, 0% supply and the box ticked; a residential building with level 1, noise Excellent and residents 1 of 1.
- **Where the Retford-Todmorden boundary is on the connecting road (player's screenshots, one game).** With the bulldozer tool active the map shows town boundaries as a heavy dashed line; it crosses the road beside the "Todmorden Maintenance Building". The three passenger vehicles the player had named (Road Vehicles 27, 22 and 23, on the line "RET-TOD Bus 1", model Landauer with 6 seats, carrying 2, 5 and 1 passengers at 20 km/h) were on the Todmorden side of that line, heading towards Todmorden. In the save the `landauer_v2` model has 20 instances; seven of them stand on that road, between about 3,600 and 4,800 m along the map's second axis (x about -860 to -1,050) and head towards Todmorden, so the three are among those seven, but the instances carry no vehicle number and which three was not told. Retford's own services (troika and `droshky` carriages) stand at about 1,430 to 1,790 on the same axis. The boundary therefore lies between those, near the maintenance building. **Observed**, one game; positions are those of a save whose clock had not moved, so the same in every save from `1428` to `1437`.

## Building records change level during play, and a record's first id is not its identity (604)

**Observed** on 604 in the Small subarctic game, saves `1700` to `1705` (clock 15.7 to 17.3 million, the player's town growing), listing the records by the pattern above and following each one by its **parcel ids** (the sorted list of the building's parcels).

- **The first id (`a`) is not a stable key.** The records of one building showed different `a` values from save to save and the values moved along neighbouring records (one `a` was 24,557 on one building in one save and 24,883 in the next, while another building took 24,557), so tracking by `a` gave wrong level histories. The parcels did not change for the same building and gave a clean history. Use the parcels to follow a building.
- **The level goes both ways within minutes.** 13 of about 660 records changed level across the six saves. One commercial record (stocked, `b` equal to `a`; 4 parcels, height 20.9 m, `timeBuilt` 0) read level 2 in `1700` and `1701`, level 1 in `1702` and `1703`, and level 2 again in `1704` and `1705`. The player watched the building across from a stop (a commercial building) go down, then up: its window read level 1 with input stocks 0 of 2 (fish) and 0 of 1 (tinned food), customers 5 of 8 and fish 0%, tinned food 0%, and later level 2 with stocks 0 of 4 and 0 of 2, customers 3 of 16, again 0% and 0%. So the level doubles the stock capacities and the customer capacity, and the level changed while both supplies were 0%. The match to this record is by the times (down by `1702`, up by `1704`, the player's windows read after `1702` and just before `1705`) and by being the only stocked four-parcel record with that history; the window did not say which record it was. A one-parcel stocked record (height 7.1 m) also went from 2 to 1 at `1703` and stayed 1.
- **Houses change level too**: seven house records (`b` = 0xffffffff) went from 1 to 2 and two from 2 to 1 in the same stretch, so a downgrade is not specific to buildings that need cargo.
- **Some records change height and build time** (the same parcels, a new `timeBuilt` close to the clock and a new height, for example 13.1 m to 24.5 m, about 800,000 units apart), which looks like a building replaced by another one on the same parcels. The level, depth and parcels stayed. About 660 records were found per save and the count grew by 3 to 10 per save.
