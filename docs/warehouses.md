# Warehouses: modules and cargo slots

Each warehouse is a construction record with a list of modules, and each module holds one cargo slot. Checked on 604, one save: catalog save [6417707](https://mod.io/g/transportfever3/m/333151) (subarctic, start year 1900) in a later manual save, compared with the game's warehouse window and build toolbar tooltips. Labels are **Observed** (one save) unless marked.

## In short

All 604; the record was read in two games and a slot filter in a re-save of a third.

- **A warehouse is one construction record (`warehouses/warehouse.con`) with a Lua table of named values, and each module in `modules` is one cargo slot (Observed, 38 and 23 warehouses).** Module types are `wh_bulk`, `wh_flatbed`, `wh_goods`, `wh_liquid` and once `wh_universal`; the window's "Specialization" is the module type, and a record holds 1 to 15 modules.
- **Capacity is 500 times the module count and the yearly upkeep is the sum over the modules (Observed, four warehouses; the per-module price depends on the date, [inflation.md](inflation.md#upkeep-and-prices-at-the-1920-step)).** A universal module costs half of a specialized one.
- **Which cargo a module is set to is not always in the record (Observed).** The first game's records had `tagToCargoType`; the second game's records had none. A player-set slot filter adds a pair to `tagToCargoType` (module key to cargo path), in ascending key order, and it survives a load and re-save. A slot the game claimed by use has no tag.
- **A full set of goods modules blocks another goods cargo (Observed, one game):** plastic filled all three goods modules and the wool lines could not unload; a wool filter on one emptied slot fixed it.
- **The stock levels and the yearly totals are not in the record;** they are differences of running totals ([statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md)). After "Discard All Cargo" the unloaded-minus-loaded stock overstates the real stock by the amount discarded.
- **Open:** where the cargo a module is set to is kept in a record without `tagToCargoType`, what the module keys encode (packed two-part positions?), what the leading entity-id list holds, what `year` and `upgrade` are, and the claiming rule for an empty goods slot, and whether "the warehouse's truck stop" in the player's account of the planks wool line ([A slot filter is saved as a tag](#a-slot-filter-is-saved-as-a-tag-604-observed-one-game)) is a `small_*` road stop or a warehouse stop (the player is not sure).

## The construction record

- Each warehouse has a record that starts with the resource path `warehouses/warehouse.con`. The save had 38 of them, and 38 entries named `... Warehouse` in the names table ([entity-names.md](entity-names.md)), so it looks like one record per warehouse.
- After the path comes a Lua table (see [lua-values.md](lua-values.md)) of named values. The key `modules` holds the modules as a table from a number to `{name, variant}`, where `name` is a path such as `::/warehouses/wh_bulk.module`. `variant` was 0 in the two records read in full. After the modules come `paramX` and `paramY` (both -0 here), `seed` and `tagToCargoType`. In a May 2000 autosave of the same game all records also had `upgrade` and `year`, and most `constructOpt56` and `constructOpt78` (see the second game below).
- `tagToCargoType` maps the same numbers to a cargo resource path, for example `::/cargos/grain/grain.cargo`. In 37 of 38 records the two tables had the same keys. In the other, a goods module (2 modules, 1 tag) had no cargo set.
- The same module type can appear many times in one warehouse. A record with 15 modules held 5 bulk, 5 flatbed, 4 goods and 1 liquid, and that is what the game's window for the matching warehouse listed: 15 stock slots, grouped under Bulk, Flatbed, Goods and Liquid headings. Records held 1 to 15 modules (counts seen: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 12, 15).
- Module types seen: `wh_bulk`, `wh_flatbed`, `wh_goods`, `wh_liquid`. The window's "Specialization" is the module type. The cargos assigned to each type were disjoint:

| Module | Cargos seen (subarctic) |
|---|---|
| `wh_bulk` | cement, coal, grain, iron_ore, sand, sawdust, stone |
| `wh_flatbed` | logs, machines, planks, sheet_metal, steel, vehicles |
| `wh_goods` | beverages, books, clothes, fish, furniture, glass, meat, plastic, tinned_food, tools, wool |
| `wh_liquid` | chemicals, crude_oil, dyes, fuel |

  The build tooltips list more icons than these (8 for bulk, 13 for goods, 6 for flatbed, 4 for liquid), so a warehouse can take more cargos than any one save used.
- The 15-module record fits the warehouse window by count and by cargo icons, as read by eye from screenshots: the goods slots were fish, plastic, books and tinned food (16, 106, 0 and 12 in the window), and the only liquid slot was fuel. One slot was checked in the game: hovering the open-book icon showed the tooltip "Books", the cargo the record tags on one of its goods modules. The match of the other slots was not checked one by one.
- The module keys are large numbers (622,502,550 to 672,502,500 seen) that differ by 40 or 50 within a warehouse, in groups. They look like packed two-part positions, since the leading digits step 62, 63, 64, ... across modules and the tail ran from 2,502,340 to 2,502,660. What they encode is **Open**.
- The module keys also occur as u32 in the entity data after the record (all 15 keys of the 15-module warehouse sat together within 4 KB, starting about 1.3 KB after the record, and again in other places 100 KB to 15 MB further on). The values next to them looked like placement data (f32 16.0 and 1.0 and a run that looks like a transform), not stock. **Observed**, one warehouse in one later save. This means the keys tie a module to its model placement, not to its stock.
- Before the path the record has a string like `__module_` plus digits and a list of entity ids (12 in the 15-module record, so not one per module). What they are is **Open**.

## Cost

- The running cost on the warehouse window was $3,750,000 a year, and the build tooltip gave $250,000 a year for each specialized module (Bulk, Flatbed, Goods, Liquid), against $125,000 for the Universal Warehouse. 15 modules times $250,000 is $3,750,000, so the yearly cost is the sum over the modules. The building prices were $150,000 (Universal) and $300,000 (specialized). **Observed**, one warehouse. How the journal books the upkeep (in batches through the year) is in [running-costs.md](running-costs.md#upkeep-and-running-costs-are-booked-in-batches-604).
- The Warehouses tab (one row per warehouse, towns have several and share names) shows Stored, Capacity, Utilization and Upkeep. For the rows compared with a record, Capacity was 500 times the module count and Upkeep $250,000 times the module count: a one-module record (a bulk coal module) read 500 and $250,000, two flatbed modules (steel and sheet metal) 1,000 and $500,000, six goods and bulk modules 3,000 and $1,500,000, and the 15-module warehouse 7,500 and $3,750,000. **Observed**, four warehouses. Stored is the sum of the slots: a six-slot warehouse with 11, 1, 11, 40, 2 and 52 showed 117.
- Noise and pollution on the tooltips: Universal 20 and 25, the specialized ones 20 and 50.

## Not found

The stock levels in each slot and the yearly incoming and outgoing totals are not in this record. They are not stored as plain numbers elsewhere either: a stock is the difference of two running totals and the yearly bars are differences of the same totals, see [statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md). Searches that failed first are in [entity-stats.md](entity-stats.md).

## Evidence and tests

### A second game: records without cargo tags

Checked on 604, catalog save [6425796](https://mod.io/g/transportfever3/m/mynewsavenotfinished1) (temperate, European names, start year 1900, map 11264 x 22528 m, no mods, shown as 18 February 1937) in a paused re-save, against the whole Warehouses tab (23 rows). **Observed**, one game.

- The save had 23 records with the path `warehouses/warehouse.con` and the tab had 23 rows. The path is followed by a Lua table whose pairs are written as tagged keys and values (see [lua-values.md](lua-values.md)); parsing pairs until a key is not a string reads it.
- Its keys were only `modules`, `seed`, `year` and, in the 14 records with more than one module, `upgrade` (the 9 one-module records had no `upgrade`). There was **no `tagToCargoType`, `paramX`, `paramY` or `constructOpt*`**, and the string `tagToCargoType` did not occur anywhere in the stream. The records of a May 2000 autosave of the first game (catalog 6417707, 604) all had `tagToCargoType`, `upgrade` and `year`, most also `paramX`, `paramY`, `constructOpt56` and `constructOpt78`. So the record's shape depends on the game, and which cargo a module is set to is **not** always in the record. The cargo a warehouse holds shows in its statistics lists instead, which matched 15 rows of this game's tab ([statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md)): the tab's icon column showed single cargos for some warehouses, for example fish only, and the record held nothing about fish while the lists held id 6. Where the cargo a module is set to (as opposed to what it holds) is kept is **Open**.
- `year` ran from 1906 to 1930 here and 1900 to 1977 in the first game (6417707), never past the game's date. It looks like the year the warehouse was built; **Open**.
- A fifth module type, `::/warehouses/wh_universal.module`, appeared once (in a 5-module record: 2 goods, flatbed, bulk, universal). The tab showed that row at capacity 2,500 and upkeep $168,750. With $37,500 a year for each specialized module that is 4 x $37,500 + $18,750, so a universal module cost half of a specialized one, as in the build tooltips above (125,000 against 250,000), and still gave 500 capacity.
- The module counts matched the tab as a group: records of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 8 modules numbered 9, 5, 3, 3, 2 and 1, and the rows with capacity 500, 1,000, 1,500, 2,000, 2,500 and 4,000 numbered the same. Capacity was 500 times the module count in every row, so the rule above held in a second game. Pairing single rows with single records was not done.
- Upkeep per specialized module was $37,500 here (1937) against $250,000 in the first game (2000), a factor of 6.7. The two games' settings differ (see [settings.md](settings.md)): `infrastructureMaintenanceScale` was 1 here (50%) and 3 in the first game (100%), which would account for a factor of 2 if warehouse upkeep follows that setting, leaving 3.3. Inflation was Low here and Normal in the first game, but that option only cuts income by rank ([inflation.md](inflation.md#the-income-multiplier)), so it does not touch upkeep.
- **The date explains the remaining 3.3** (**Observed**, 604). In a third game the warehouse read $50,000 in 1919 and $75,000 on 1 January 1920, and 50,000 per specialized module makes both figures here exact. The table, the readings and what was not checked are in [inflation.md](inflation.md#upkeep-and-prices-at-the-1920-step).

### A full goods space blocks another goods cargo (604, Observed, one game)

In catalog save [6425796](https://mod.io/g/transportfever3/m/mynewsavenotfinished1) a warehouse with three goods modules (500 each) held plastic only, 1,500 of 1,500 in the window, and the wool lines that deliver to it could not unload. The warehouse's statistics group ([statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md#stock--unloaded-minus-loaded)) showed how it came about: plastic stock cycled between 964 and 1,012 for years (two modules' worth), wool went in and out of the third (stock 0), and a few hundred thousand clock units after the last wool left, plastic passed 1,000 and went on to 1,500. The game then reported, for the wool line, that a cargo "cannot be unloaded anywhere" ([notifications.md](notifications.md#params-by-type)). So a goods module with no stock seems to be claimed by whichever goods cargo arrives next, and a full set of modules leaves other goods nowhere to go. The claiming rule itself is **Open**; the player can stop it with a slot filter, below. The worked reading is in [analysis-examples/clothes-train2-supply-chain-starvation-6425796.md](analysis-examples/clothes-train2-supply-chain-starvation-6425796.md).

### A slot filter is saved as a tag (604, Observed, one game)

In a re-save of the same game ([6425796](https://mod.io/g/transportfever3/m/mynewsavenotfinished1), loaded in the game and saved the same day) the player used "Discard All Cargo" on one of that warehouse's three full plastic modules, which left an empty goods slot with a filter icon. Clicking the slot opened a row of 13 goods icons to pick one cargo from. The player chose `wool` and saved.

- **The record gained a key.** Of the 23 records, only the 5-module one (3 goods, liquid, flatbed) now had `tagToCargoType`, with one pair: a module key mapped to `::/cargos/wool/wool.cargo`. The key was the last goods module's, the largest in the record; the window listed the filtered slot first among the goods slots, so the window's order is not the key order. Its module table, `seed`, `year` and `upgrade` were unchanged, and no other record changed.
- **So a player-set filter is in the record and a claimed slot is not.** The other two goods modules still held plastic and had no tag; nothing in the record says what a slot holds unless it was filtered. This answers part of the question above: a record without `tagToCargoType` has no filtered slot.
- **The discard is in no statistics list.** Right after the save, the warehouse's plastic group still read 4,908 unloaded and 3,408 loaded (1,500 in stock by the rule in [statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md#stock--unloaded-minus-loaded)) while the window showed 1,000 in two modules, and no list name was new. In a second save 63,200 clock units later (same session, no load in between) the group read 4,913 and 3,420, so 1,493, against 993 in the window: exactly the 500 discarded. After a discard, unloaded minus loaded overstates the stock by the amount discarded (**Observed**; the other cargos still matched: planks 3,430 - 3,278 = 152, fuel 2,792 - 2,304 = 488).
- **Wool then arrived in the filtered slot.** The group's `itemsUnloaded25` ended at 1,324 (last entry 94,374,200, after the first save at 94,328,800) against `itemsLoaded25` 1,312, and the window showed 12 of 500 in the wool slot. The wool line that had been blocked unloaded again, so the filter fixed the blockage (**Observed**, one game). The plastic modules went on taking plastic (the stock moved from 1,000 to 993), so this save does not show whether a filtered slot refuses other goods.
- **The tag survives a load and re-save, and the game still applies it.** The second save was loaded and saved again with the game paused (same newest list time): the record still had the one pair, and the warehouse group's lists were unchanged.
- **A second filter adds a second pair, and a slot that holds goods can be filtered.** Clicking the cargo icon of a plastic slot with 496 in it (a second goods module) set its filter to the cargo it already held; the stock stayed and the icon gained the funnel. The record then had two pairs: the first goods module's key mapped to `::/cargos/plastic/plastic.cargo` and the wool pair as before. The pairs are in ascending key order, not the order they were made in (wool came first). The window lists the filtered slots first and the unfiltered one (497) last, so its order is not the table's order either. The game was quit, started again and the save loaded and re-saved: the two pairs were unchanged, and the warehouse group's lists were the same in both saves (**Observed**, one game). The third goods module, with no tag, still holds plastic by use.
- **After a run of 240,800 clock units from the filter saves (604, Observed, one game).** The warehouse group's wool lists went to 1,377 unloaded and 1,373 loaded: 53 in and 61 out, so wool now passes through the warehouse and the filtered slot holds 4. The plastic lists read 4,944 and 3,444, 1,500 minus the discarded 500 is 1,000, exactly the two plastic slots' capacity, while the wool slot had about 490 free; so plastic did not spill into the filtered slot (the window itself was not read in this save). The tags were unchanged.
- **A longer run (autosave at clock 95,447,200, 1.05 million units after the filter saves).** Wool lists 1,507 unloaded and 1,489 loaded (stock 18; the window, read a little later, showed 20 of 500, planks 276 as the lists say). Plastic 5,016 and 3,540, so 976 after the 500 discard, and the window showed 1,000 of 1,000 plastic with the wool slot at 20 of 500: plastic filled its two slots and stayed at the cap while about 480 wool-slot places were free. The last plastic unloaded was 227,000 units before the save while plastic loads went on, so plastic stopped entering once full (**Observed**, one game; whether it waited outside is not read).
- **Line warnings.** Between the catalog save and the first re-save, at the same clock (94,328,800), the stored warning entries for the two wool lines were gone and other entries were stamped at that clock; whether the load, the discard or the filter caused it is not separable ([notifications.md](notifications.md#params-by-type) for the entry layout). The line windows read, at the autosave, "A cargo type cannot be unloaded anywhere, despite being loaded at stop 1" with Cargo Type Planks on Wool Zachod 1 (the entry from clock 80,402,400, cargo 13) and the same text for stop 2 with Cargo Type Meat on the five-cargo wool line (cargo 7, stamped 94,328,800), neither about wool. The player's guess at the planks one (the line was built by someone else): it once delivered planks to a saw mill it still drives past, and now runs only between the warehouse's truck stop and a wool farm (whether that is a `small_*` road stop or a warehouse stop is **Open**), so its stop-1 planks configuration has no unloading stop (the line's stops were not read from the save, [lines.md](line-ids.md); not tested). A truck of the main wool line (Road Vehicle 256) raised a new `cargo_rtc_warning` 82,000 units after the filter save, so an unload problem was not gone for good. The player's reading is that a line's two stops are both unforced ("Force Unload All Cargo On Arrival" off, [lines.md](lines.md#the-stop-configuration-record)), so a problem at either end affects the whole line and its marker can last until every vehicle has run a circuit (not tested).
