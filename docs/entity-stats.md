# Stocks and yearly figures: searches that failed

What was looked for in the entity data and not found. Checked on 604, catalog save [6417707](https://mod.io/g/transportfever3/m/333151) (paused on 17 April 2000, **A**) and a later manual save of the same game, still paused when screenshots of the game were taken (**B**). A failed search shows how the values are not stored, not where they are; the items that did show something carry a label. The figures were later found as differences of running totals, see [statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md); this note keeps what was tried before that.

Numbers read from the game's windows, then searched for in the decompressed stream:

| Value shown | Where | Searched as |
|---|---|---|
| A vehicle's and a line's income for a year (for example 15,767,238 and 24,549,162) and last-year totals | Vehicle and line windows, Balance chart tooltips | i64, f64 and f32 of the shown number, also scaled by 100, 1,000, 0.01 and 0.001; f64 anywhere in the shown number to the next whole number. The scaled searches for 0.01 and 0.001 gave a few i64 hits on 5 and 6 digit numbers (6 to 280 each) that were not examined and are probably chance |
| A construction site's input stocks (0, 56, 116 and 97 against capacities 150, 150, 300 and 150) | Landmark construction site window | The four values with their capacities close together, as u16, u32, f32 and f64, in A and B |
| A warehouse's ten stock levels (477, 16, 64, 30, 12, 53, 49, 392, 12 and 106, each of 500) | Warehouse window | The values close together as u16, u32, i64, f32 and f64, scaled by 10, 100, 256 and 1,000, and as the exact sequence in window order and reverse with up to four zero entries between |
| A warehouse's yearly incoming and outgoing (2,640 for 1998, 2,861 and 2,722) | Statistics chart tooltips | Together as u32, i64, f32 and f64. The one hit was a long sorted run of increasing integers, a false positive |

- The Lua tables of the script states did hold the figures that sit in them, for example the construction progress per cargo in [landmarks.md](landmarks.md) and the subsidy amounts in [script-states.md](script-states.md). So the figures not found are most likely in the binary entity data, in a form these searches did not cover: yearly figures may be kept in finer buckets (the shown number a sum), and stocks may be in a packed or per-entity layout.
- Searching for small numbers or for entity ids gives many false hits: ids and counters are dense in long sorted lists and in repeating `(tag, value)` runs near the end of the stream. A search needs a rare value (a large money figure) or several values that must agree.
- A warehouse is built from modules, one per cargo slot, see [warehouses.md](warehouses.md). So stocks may be kept per module rather than per warehouse, which a search for ten values close together would miss. A search per module found nothing either (below), and the stock turned out to be per cargo, not per module ([statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md)).
- A third save of the same game (paused later, with 8 of the warehouse's stock levels changed: 460, 208, 108, 83, 60, 34, 15 and 8) was searched for those eight values together as u16, u32, f32, f64 and i64 within 200, 2,000 and 20,000 bytes. Nothing clustered within 200 bytes. The wider windows gave thousands of hits on small numbers and two f32 hits that were scattered floats (probably other float arrays), so no stock was found.
- A fourth save (24 game days later, again paused) changed 8 of the 12 visible levels and left 4 unchanged (208, 8, 49 and 15). The streams were 3.1 MB apart in length, so they can't be compared as a whole. Aligned on the 15-module warehouse record, the stream was byte-identical from 71 KB before it to 109 KB after it, and drifted apart on both sides (moving vehicles are stored as floats of variable length, so any change shifts what follows). So the warehouse's stocks, which had changed, are not in the 180 KB around its construction record. **Observed**, one warehouse in two saves.
- A search for seven of the new levels together found only two u16 hits within 512 bytes, both in a dense run of small u16 values near 928 MB (groups of four values with 15360, the half-float 1.0, in every fourth place). Almost any value under 500 occurs in that run, so these hits mean nothing. The same region gave the one `x256` hit in the second save.
- Each module's own stock (read from the window, in the fourth save) was searched within 48 bytes either side of every u32 occurrence of that module's key (see [warehouses.md](warehouses.md)), as u16, u32, f32 and f64. Nothing consistent turned up: grain (473), stone (362), cement, plastic, steel, coal, sheet metal and iron ore had no hit at all, and the few hits on small values (logs 8, books 15, tinned food 14) were one-offs at unrelated places. So a module's stock is not stored next to its key as a plain number.
- A quieter warehouse gave two exact values (a flatbed warehouse with steel at 411 and sheet metal at 420, in a sixth save). Searched together within 32, 256 and 2,048 bytes as u16, u32, i64, f32 and f64: the integer widths gave thousands of hits (dense number-heavy regions) and f32 none. The four f64 hits had the same spacing (1,890 bytes) in four places 150 to 250 KB apart, so they are one repeated array, not this warehouse. The same warehouse type with 0 and 0 (a second steel and sheet metal warehouse) is a built-in control for any candidate.
- The construction site table in [landmarks.md](landmarks.md) stores amounts as `cargo id -> amount`. Five known (cargo id, amount) pairs from a six-slot warehouse (meat 7 with 1, cement 15 with 11, clothes 27 with 40, tinned food 28 with 2 and furniture 31 with 52) were searched as an id followed directly by an amount, in all 25 combinations of id (u8, u16, u32, u64, f64) and amount (u16, u32, u64, f32, f64) widths, needing three of the other four within 160 bytes. No hits.
- A seventh save (paused, a warehouse empty and a ship with 30 chemicals on its way to the station beside it) gave a full yearly series: the warehouse window's incoming bars for 1985 to 2000 (120, 120, about 117, 120, 120, 90, 135, 135, 90, 150, 90, 120, 120, 120, 120, 60) matched the station window's unloaded bars exactly, so the two windows show one count. Searched as the whole series (the third value a wildcard), and as the ten bars for 1990 to 1999 alone, forwards and in reverse, as u8, u16, u32, u64, f32 and f64 with 0 to 3 elements between them, and as Lua numbers (tag 2 and f64) back to back or with a key between: no hits. So the yearly bars are not stored as a plain list of yearly totals. **Observed**, one warehouse.
- The same save's ship showed a last-year income of $226,637 and a running cost of $120,701 a year. The income gave no i64, f64 or f32 hit (also x100 and x1,000) and 9 u32 hits, and the running cost times 1,000 gave 11 hits as u32 and i64. All of them sat inside runs of ascending 8-byte values (around 0x0373_0000 for the income and 0x0720_0000 for the running cost, so probably a table of offsets or sizes), so they are chance. A last-year income of $2,531 for a line gave hundreds of hits on small numbers and was not examined.
- A before and after sequence was done with three paused saves: **P** (a warehouse empty, a ship on its way with 30 chemicals), **Q** (the ship half unloaded: warehouse 17, ship 13) and **R** (unloaded: warehouse 30, ship 0; the warehouse window's incoming bar for the year went from 60 to 90, and the station's unloaded bar followed). About 41 game days lay between P and Q, so a great deal else moved. The streams were aligned one 4,096-byte block at a time (each block of the earlier save found in the later one by 40-byte anchors, several per block, within 64 KB of the previous offset). About 8% of the blocks could not be aligned in P to Q, and about 20% in Q to R, so the test covers most of the stream, not all of it. Positions where the earlier save held 0 and the later one 17 (and 17 then 30), or 30 then 13 then 0, as u16, u32, u64, f32 or f64, were intersected over the two pairs: ten hits for the warehouse and none for the ship, all inside lists of ascending 8-byte numbers, where a value like 0x11 is just part of an id. So a stock is not a plain number that changes in place at a place that could be aligned. **Observed**, one warehouse in three saves.
- **Lists of event times (Observed, found by the stock search).** The stretch holds many named lists (`itemsUnloaded0`, `itemsLoaded`, `itemsTransported`, `itemsAtEdge0_c0`, ...): times in the game clock unit and running totals, laid out as in [statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md). A first reading took the `itemsUnloaded0` list that gained one entry between the three paused saves for the ship's list; it was not: the 0 in the name is a cargo id that nearly every stop-like group has, and the ship's cargo was id 19. The yearly bars were first compared with the number of entries per year in each list (best correlation 0.88, no match). That was the wrong comparison: the bars are differences of the running totals at year ends, which matched. A time like 147,216,600 sitting in 281 places is likewise explained by many lists changing on the same day in a busy game.
- **Who the lists belong to (Open).** The groups (`u32` key, `u32` field count, fields) and their keys are described in [statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md). Ties from a group to a named station, ship or warehouse were not found, only to a warehouse window by its figures.
- Not tried: a save of a map with one warehouse and nothing else, which would make the entity's data easy to isolate.
