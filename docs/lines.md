# Lines and stop configuration

Where a line's stops keep their load and departure settings, and what a new line leaves in a save. Trip-plan records are in [trip-plans.md](trip-plans.md). Checked on 604 in one new game, two saves made minutes apart (`1246`, then `1312` with two new ship lines and edited stops), compared with the Configure Stop windows.

## The stop configuration record

**Confirmed** (604): every stop on a line has a record, found by this pattern:

| Field | Type | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 37 x | f32 | 1.0 in every record seen. **Open** (it is not the cargo load percentage, which is 100% in the window for a fish stop and absent for the stop that only unloads, and the floats are the same in both) |
| 3 | u8 x 3 | Flags: the first is "Force Unload All Cargo On Arrival" (0 or 1); the other two were 1 in every record, and are the window's "Destroy Cargo On Arrival When Config Changes" and "Replace with Newer Cargo if Available", both ticked by default (which byte is which was not told apart) |
| 3 | f32 x 3 | Minimum stop time, maximum stop time, maximum additional wait, in seconds |

The window lists them in the order Max. Additional Wait, Min. Stop Time, Max. Stop Time; the record stores min, max, wait.

- **Defaults.** A road line's stops held 0, 600, 30 (20 stops in the first save, so the window's default maximum stop time is 10 minutes and the additional wait 30 s), a ship line's 0, 600, 60 (the two stops of the fishing line).
- **One change per stop, tested in the game.** Two new fishing lines each had a fishing stop (loading fish, 100%) and the port stop. On the first the port stop had "Force Unload All Cargo On Arrival" ticked: its record is the only new one with the first flag byte 1 (`01 01 01`), and 4 of the old road stops have it too. On the second the port stop had custom times, minimum 3 s, maximum 3 min 48 s and additional wait 50 s: its record holds the floats 3.0, 228.0 and 50.0. The player made both edits and the window values match the bytes exactly.
- **Pattern.** 37 f32 values of 1.0, then three bytes (`00` or `01`, then `01 01`), then three floats between 0 and 3,600 that are not all 0. A looser pattern (any three flag bytes) gave over 1,200 false hits in the first save, almost all with zero floats, so check the flags and the floats. In the first save it found 22 records (nine lines), in the second 26: four new stops on two new lines.
- **What follows the three floats** differs by stop. The loading stops of all three fishing lines were followed by the same 4 bytes (`59 6b 00 00`), the port stops by `00 10 00 00 01 00 00 80 3f` and then a short list. The load list of that game was not decoded; a per-cargo byte vector at the end of the record is the candidate, see [line-ids.md](line-ids.md).
- **The flag flipped on two existing stops (604, Confirmed in the game, one game).** A game with three fishing lines had the flag set on one port stop only. The player ticked it on the port stops of the other two, and the next manual save, about a million clock units after the last autosave, held `01` in both records and no other record changed its flag; the autosave before it still held `00` there. Nothing else in those two records changed except as noted next. Three saves in between, with other edits, left every flag byte as it was.
- **The four bytes after the three floats, on a ship port stop (604, Observed, one game).** The three port stops of that game held 3, 2 and 1 in the first byte of the four bytes that follow the `01 00 00 80 3f`, and the last one went from 1 to 2 while the player was changing that port's stops; a road record's went 2 to 3 when a stop was added. What it counts is **Open** (stops or terminals tied to the stop?), as no change was tied to a single edit. An earlier rebuild of the same port, to three stops, had replaced that stop's custom times (3 s, 3 min 48 s, 50 s) with the defaults, 0, 600 and 60, so a stop that is removed and made again loses its custom times.
- **A stop added to a road line (604, Observed, one game).** A third stop with force unload ticked gave one more record (549 against 548) with flag 1 and the defaults 0, 600, 30. The first stop's flag flipped to 1 and the third's stayed 0. File order is not the line's stop order.
- **The departure option is a byte before the record (604, Confirmed in the game, one stop).** Changing one road stop through the three icons gave 0 (first, "Load if Available"), 1 (second, "Full Load (Any)") and 2 (third, "Full Load (All)") in the byte 53 before the record's 37 floats. The three floats followed the window each time (10.0, 193.0, 40.0, then 3.0, 283.0, 85.0), so the option does not change them. The byte is 0 in 541 of 549 records and 2 in three (stops set to "Full Load (All)" is a reading, not checked).
- **Terminal use on a stop (604, Observed, one game).** The "Select Terminals" window lists a stop's terminals, each Preferred, Alternative or Don't Use (the Preferred one is greyed and cannot be changed; at one warehouse stop the Liquid and Flatbed terminals were Don't Use and Goods the Preferred one). The saw mill stop had three terminals: the first Preferred, the others Alternative. Setting the second to Don't Use changed the stop's tail, the words after its first four bytes (a number that stays the same, here 284,325): `0, 0, 2, 0, 1, 0, 2, 0, 0` became `0, 0, 1, 0, 2, 0, 0`. That reads as a count (2, then 1) and `0, terminal number counted from 0` pairs for the Alternative terminals, with Don't Use dropping its pair (unverified: another stop's words did not fit the pattern, and the stream was 160 bytes shorter, 8 of them here). Which place holds Don't Use is **Open**.
- **Offsets move.** The records sit near 6.9 MB into a 79 MB stream, in a run ahead of the Lua states and the entity names. Find them by the pattern.

## A new line and its vehicles

**Observed** (604, `1246` against `1312`).

- Each of the two new lines was a fishing stop, then the port stop. The 37 floats of the fishing record and of the port record were 232 bytes apart; the port record of the first new line ran 13 bytes longer before the next line's records (245). Why is **Open**.
- The journal gained 13 `Vehicle purchase (Water)` bookings at the paused clock (5,997,000): 5 of -438,912 (Ship 7 to 11, on the first new line) and 8 of -256,032 (Ship 12 to 19, on the second). Their sum, 4,242,816, is the fall in the account between the saves. The first build-out's four small ships were 4 x 256,032 (the journal merges them to one -1,024,128). So the line with the larger ships paid 438,912 each and the line with the small ones 256,032 each. The larger ship cost 438,891 earlier in the same game ([finances.md](finances.md#a-new-games-journal)) and 438,912 here, 21 more, which was not explained.
- The names of the new lines and ships were appended to the entity names table, line first, see [entity-names.md](entity-names.md#renaming-industries-and-a-new-line-with-its-ships-604).
- The new lines' stops reuse the existing port stop. Nothing was added to the names table for them.

## The Lines window's Frequency, Rate and Load columns (604)

**Observed** in the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game): 34 readings of the Lines tab and line windows from clock 54.6 to 67.7 million (December 1916 to March 1919), 13 lines on one tab plus two road lines read again at 19 dates, before, during and after their vehicles were replaced. The lines' arrivals were read from the statistics lists ([cement-chain.md](cement-chain.md#replacing-both-lines-vehicles-604)). One game second is 1,000 clock units ([calendar.md](calendar.md)).

- **Rate = capacity x 1,461 / (vehicles x Frequency in seconds)**, shown rounded, with Frequency shown cut off to whole seconds. All 34 readings fit, buses, ships and trucks alike: for example 36 x 1,461 / (9 x 112 s) = 52.2, shown 52, and 168 x 1,461 / (14 x 339 s) = 51.7, shown 52. A model that cuts off the Rate as well fits 33 of the 34 and fails that last one. So Rate is the capacity the line moves per 1,461 game seconds, which is 1,461,000 clock units, the period of the yearly chart bars ([statistics-lists.md](statistics-lists.md#the-yearly-bars-are-periods-of-1461000-units-604)), whatever the calendar speed.
- **Frequency is the line's round trip divided by its vehicle count, and it follows the trips actually completed.** The stone line showed 112 s with 9 carts (`horsewagon_1850_usa_v2` and `horse_cart_small`), and its stone arrivals at the plant were 112,877 units apart on average; the cement delivery line showed 2 min 31 s with 23 trucks (3,473 s), and its bunched trucks came back every 3,454,000 units. After seven trucks replaced the stone line's carts the figure went 118 s (no truck had finished a loop yet), 95 s, 94 s, and the loops measured in the lists averaged 666 s, which is 95.2 s for seven trucks. With old and new vehicles on the line together it read 63 s for 16 vehicles, 1,008 s, the carts' loop. Bunching does not change it, as an average. Once the stone trucks had spread out evenly again (loops of 628,000 to 633,000 units, 89.7 to 90.4 s a truck) the window read 89 s, and the Rate shown (195) needs a Frequency of 89.7 to 90; the delivery line, running as two convoys with round trips of about 2.8 to 2.9 million units, read 108 s for 27 trucks (2,916 s). Which completed loops it averages is **Open**: the shown figure ran up to about 5% above the loops measured up to each reading.
- **A line whose vehicles wait for cargo did not fit.** The fish truck line showed 27 s with 7 vehicles, but its arrivals at the first stop were 48,680 units apart. Its trucks load at a warehouse that was empty at the time, so time spent waiting for fish is a likely reason; that the column leaves waiting time out is **Open**.
- **Load is the cargo aboard the line's vehicles.** It matched the cargo the lists put in transit (30 and 23 against 30 and 24 on the two cement lines). When the delivery line's old trucks were sent to the depot and sold, the lists still counted 140 cement in transit and the window showed 110, the cargo on the new trucks ([cement-chain.md](cement-chain.md#replacing-both-lines-vehicles-604)). In March 1919 the two lines read 96 and 49 where the lists counted 126 and 72: 30 and 23 left on vehicles that were sold, and the rest matched exactly (on the stone line 3 trucks of 12 stone, 1 stone still being unloaded at the plant, and 2 trucks of 6 cement).
