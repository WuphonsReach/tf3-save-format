# Trip plans: line, stops and vehicle, or a building (604)

**Observed** on 604 in the [Small subarctic game](test-games.md#small-subarctic-game), saves `1437` to `1456`, checked against the player's screenshots of the vehicle windows. A bus line of two stops (the stops were named George Street, in the first town, and Highfield Road, in the second) carried 80 vehicles' worth of records in `1438`. An earlier version of this note read the records as the vehicle's own leg, and a later one as one record per passenger aboard; the saves `1446` to `1456` show that a record outlives the ride, and that who owns it is not settled (below).

- **The records.** An array of records, each five `u32`: the line's entity id, or `0xffffffff`; two small numbers `a` and `b`; a vehicle's entity id; and 0. It held 423 records in every save from `1437` to `1444` and 451 in `1445` (clock 13,466,600). Find it by searching for a line's entity id (from a notification's `simParams.mapping`, [notifications.md](notifications.md)) followed by two small numbers, then a vehicle id and a zero, and step back and forward by 20 bytes while the shape holds.
- **Counts matched the windows only while the vehicles were freshly loaded.** The player's windows against the records of the same vehicle: in `1438`, Road Vehicle 22 showed 5 of 6 aboard and had 5 records, Road Vehicle 23 showed 1 of 6 and had 1, Road Vehicle 27 showed 2 of 6 and had 2 (windows at 15:08, after the save; `1439`, made at 15:10, had the same clock, so the game had not moved on). In `1445`, Road Vehicles 19, 20 and 21 and 27 each showed 6 of 6 and each had 6 records. Later saves showed that this was a coincidence of the moment, not the rule (next bullet).
- **A record stays after its person has left the vehicle, until it is rewritten (604, Observed, one game, `1445` to `1455`).** Road Vehicle 19 held six riders in `1445` to `1447` (clock 13,466,600) and six matching records. In `1448` (clock 13,546,000) it showed 2 of 6 and the array held 8 records with its vehicle id: the six old ones, unchanged byte for byte although those people had left the bus, and two new ones for the two who had boarded. In `1449` (27,400 units later) all six old records changed within the same interval, each to a new plan. Three people who left an `american_post_coach` (Stagecoach) at one stop (`1451`) and the rider who left it at the next (`1452`) showed the same: boarding, riding, staying aboard and getting off did not change a record. Group sizes also pass the vehicle's capacity: in `1450` one `landauer_v2`'s id appeared in 8 records of line 29568 and another's in 7 of line 27566 (capacity 6), and an `american_post_coach` with 4 aboard matched no group of 4. So the number of records with one vehicle id is not the number aboard. The array held 451 records in every save from `1445` to `1454` and 455 in `1455`; what makes records come and go (423 before `1445`) is **Open**.

## Records without a line

- **Records without a line are visits: the last field is a building (604, Observed, `1449` to `1454`).** 306 of the 451 records in `1449` had `0xffffffff` for the line and both stops. Their last field repeats: 41 distinct values in `1449`, the most common ones exactly 20 records each. In `1452` no record carried building 33628; in `1453` (clock 13,746,400, 66,800 units later) exactly 20 did, and the player had a window open on a commercial building whose customer list read 16 of 16, some "Currently Here" and some "On the Way". So the field is the building a person is in or heading for, and a building takes up to 20 such records. Why the window's 16 and the array's 20 differ is **Open**. The building ids of these records are not the ids of the town building records ([town-buildings.md](town-buildings.md#the-building-record-and-historic-preservation-604)); in `1458` the 44 ids of the array and the 660 building records shared none. Those 20 records did not change in `1454` (clock 13,918,800, 172,400 units later), when the player's tracked customer had already left the shop and was walking back out front, so leaving a building does not rewrite the record either.

## The stops a and b

- **`a` and `b` are the stops a passenger rides between**, counted in the line's stop order. On the two-stop line they are `(0, 1)` (boarded at the first stop, going to the second) or `(1, 0)`. Check against the player's description of `1445`: Road Vehicles 19 and 20, on their way to the second town's stop (20 approaching it on another street segment, 19 further back), read `(0, 1)` on all six records; Road Vehicle 21, standing at that stop with 6 of 6 loaded for the trip back, read `(1, 0)`; Road Vehicle 27, just out of the first stop with six, read `(0, 1)`. The three vehicles the player tracked on the connecting road (22, 23 and 27) read `(0, 1)` in `1437` to `1439`, driving towards the second town. In `1438`, 56 of the line's 80 records read `(0, 1)` and 24 `(1, 0)`; in `1440`, 462 s later, 12 and 74.
- **On lines with more stops** (an 18-record line in `1438`) the pairs were `(3, 1)`, `(1, 2)`, `(0, 3)`, `(1, 3)`, `(3, 0)`, `(2, 0)`, `(0, 2)`, `(1, 0)`, `(0, 1)`: not consecutive stops, which fits one pair per trip plan (origin, destination). Whether the passengers of one vehicle can differ was not checked.

## Which person a record belongs to

- **A person's identity is not in the record, and the link is still open.** The record holds no person. A passenger's window (name, home town, work town, shop, happiness, current route "Home", line, "Work") was opened for one of the six aboard Road Vehicle 27, who lives in the first town and works in the second, which agrees with `(0, 1)`. The names are in the entity names table ([entity-names.md](entity-names.md)); two of the six names occurred in two slots each, so a name alone does not give the person. The passenger lists of Road Vehicles 19, 20 and 21 in `1445` (18 more names, windows at 15:55 to 15:56) read the same way: every name was in the table, in slots from 27 to 2,358 with no clustering by vehicle, 5 of the 18 in two slots, and all were shown Happy. Two of the 18 lacked an icon (one the shop, one the home), so a passenger need not have all three locations. The player's reading of why the game keeps these records: a trip is a walk to the stop, the ride, and a walk from the last stop, all of which take time and lower a passenger's happiness (a larger topic, not tested here). A rename test tried to find it: the player renamed riders of one bus (`1446`, `1447`; a rename replaces the string in its table slot and nothing else changes, [entity-names.md](entity-names.md#renaming-people-in-place-604)) and followed one of them through `1448` to `1455`. Of the six records the bus held in `1447`, three turned into plans on a second line when he waited for it (`1449`), and when he went into a shop in `1453` exactly one of those three (index 211 in a 451-record array) changed to a record for that shop, which made it the candidate. It then failed: in `1455` (clock 13,976,600), with the same person waiting at a stop for a ride home on the second line (his window read Shop, line, Home), none of the six records had changed, and no record had gone from that shop to a bus. The 20 shop records were the same in `1453`, `1454` and `1455`. So the six records were not his, or a visit record does not follow the person who made it. The earlier guess (index 367, a ride home on the first line) had been wrong too. In `1456` (clock 13,992,600) he boarded Road Vehicle 72, whose window then read 6 of 6; six records had moved to a second-line plan in the previous save, as many as were aboard, but none of them had been a shop or bus record at the earlier points of his trip. A search of all 451 records over `1445` to `1456` for one whose line or building field followed his reported states (ride on the first line, wait for and ride the second, a shop, wait again and ride the second again, allowing a save of lag) found none. So a record is not one person's running state. The link from a person to a record is **Open**, and the array may not be keyed by person at all. A name slot does not map to a record index by any rule found, and nothing at a record's neighbours or in the person's other table entries carries a name slot or changes with his state.

## The next stop, a depot order and a second list

- **What the window shows as the line's second row is the next stop.** Road Vehicle 27 read "George Street" while heading for the first town and "Highfield Road" after loading there; Road Vehicles 19, 20 and 21 read "Highfield Road" as they came in. The stops are entities with the names of their streets; a stop window listed the line with 36 passengers waiting at George Street and a capacity of 50 ("36 / 50"). Where the waiting count is stored is **Open**.
- **Sending a vehicle to a depot** (Road Vehicle 27, `1439`, saved at the same clock as `1438` right after the order): its two records were unchanged. It was gone from the array in `1440` and after, and its entity id then occurred only in `happiness_vehicle` entries until `1445`. Its model instance was dropped and made again on the way ([vehicles.md](vehicles.md#vehicle-instances-604)); the records follow the passengers, not the instance.
- **A second list.** Another array of `[u64 clock][u64 vehicle id][u32 1 to 4]` entries (111 in `1441` and in `1445`) holds the same vehicle ids, four entries per vehicle (one with each state value), two of them within about 100,000 units of the save's clock and two 1.3 to 1.8 million after it. What it schedules is **Open**. Road Vehicle 27's entries were gone between `1439` and `1440` and its id was back in it in `1445`. One match (a state-1 entry 4,600 units before the clock for the bus that had just left a stop) did not repeat for the other vehicles, so it is not a departure time.
